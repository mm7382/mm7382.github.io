Zi Wei Dou Shu vs Bazi: how they differ and which to learn first

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-10-15

Zi Wei Dou Shu and Bazi are the two Chinese metaphysical systems people mention most. Both need your birth time, yet they organise that information in completely different ways. This article compares only their methods, not which is "more accurate", to help you decide where to start.

What is the biggest difference between Zi Wei Dou Shu and Bazi?

The biggest difference is what each turns your birth time into. Bazi converts the year, month, day and hour of birth into four pairs of heavenly stems and earthly branches, eight characters in all, and analyses the Five Phases relationships among them. Zi Wei Dou Shu places many stars into twelve palaces according to your birth time, producing a diagram that is read palace by palace. Put simply, Bazi is like reading the relationships within a set of symbols; Zi Wei Dou Shu is like reading a map divided into areas.

Do they use the same calendar?

Not exactly. Bazi is based on the solar-term calendar: the month pillar changes at the solar terms and the year pillar usually at the Beginning of Spring, not on Chinese New Year. A Zi Wei chart is built mainly from the lunar month and day and the birth hour, with leap months handled by whichever rule is adopted. So the same birthday can fall in a different "month" in each system.

How is Bazi read?

Bazi centres on the Day Master, the heavenly stem of the day pillar. You look at how the other stems and branches relate to it through the Five Phases cycles: same as me, produced by me, controlled by me, controlling me, producing me. These are refined into the Ten Gods, and interactions such as stem combinations and the six branch combinations and clashes come later. Its core tools are stems, branches, yin and yang, and the Five Phases.

How is Zi Wei Dou Shu read?

A Zi Wei chart has twelve palaces, such as Life, Siblings, Spouse, Wealth and Career, each standing for an area of life. Reading starts in the Life Palace: which major stars are there, then the related palaces known as the "three directions and four positions". Time layers include decade periods and yearly periods. Its core tools are palaces and stars.

What is each like to learn?

  • Bazi: fewer basic parts to memorise (ten stems, twelve branches and the Five Phases), but many combinations, so you need to practise deriving relationships.
  • Zi Wei Dou Shu: many parts, including twelve palaces and fourteen major stars among others, but the chart is a diagram with clearly separated areas of life, which many find intuitive.
  • Both need a birth time, and an uncertain time affects different parts of each.

Which should a beginner start with?

There is no single answer; choose by how you like to learn:

  • If you enjoy deriving conclusions from a few rules and are drawn to yin, yang and the Five Phases, start with Bazi.
  • If you like visual layouts and understanding life one area at a time, start with Zi Wei Dou Shu.

They also share foundations, such as stems, branches and the Five Phases, which carry over when you move from one to the other.

Can you learn both at once?

You can, but it helps to get one system's structure solid first. Their terminology differs and is easy to mix up: Bazi's year pillar and Zi Wei's yearly period, for example, are entirely different ideas.

FAQ

Do Zi Wei Dou Shu and Bazi need the same birth details?
Both need the year, month, day and time of birth. Bazi converts them with the solar-term calendar into four pillars; Zi Wei builds the chart from the lunar month and day and the birth hour.
Can a Bazi chart be converted into a Zi Wei chart?
Not directly. The two use different components and methods, so one cannot be turned into the other.
Is Zi Wei easier after learning Bazi?
Usually it is easier to get started, because stems, branches and the Five Phases are shared foundations, but the palaces and stars still need to be learned separately.
