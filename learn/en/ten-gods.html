What are the Ten Gods? Reading relationships in a Bazi chart from the Day Master

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-10-15

The Ten Gods are the vocabulary Bazi uses for relationships. Starting from the Day Master, you look at how each of the other stems relates to "you" and give that relationship a name. Once you know the Ten Gods, a chart stops being eight characters and becomes a set of relationships.

Who is at the centre of the Ten Gods?

The Day Master, the heavenly stem of the day pillar. Every other stem in the chart, and the hidden stems inside the branches, is compared with the Day Master to see which relationship it represents.

That means the same character can be a different Ten God in different people's charts.

How are the Ten Gods derived?

You need only two things: the Five Phases relationship, and whether the polarity (yin or yang) is the same. There are five phase relationships, each split in two by polarity, giving ten:

  • Same phase as me: same polarity is the Friend (Bi Jian); different polarity is the Rob Wealth (Jie Cai).
  • I generate it: same polarity is the Eating God (Shi Shen); different is the Hurting Officer (Shang Guan).
  • I control it: same polarity is Indirect Wealth (Pian Cai); different is Direct Wealth (Zheng Cai).
  • It controls me: same polarity is the Seven Killings (Qi Sha, also Indirect Officer); different is the Direct Officer (Zheng Guan).
  • It generates me: same polarity is the Indirect Resource (Pian Yin); different is the Direct Resource (Zheng Yin).

Can we work through an example?

Suppose the Day Master is Jia, yang Wood. By the rules above, the ten stems relate to Jia as follows:

  • Jia: Friend; Yi: Rob Wealth
  • Bing: Eating God; Ding: Hurting Officer
  • Wu: Indirect Wealth; Ji: Direct Wealth
  • Geng: Seven Killings; Xin: Direct Officer
  • Ren: Indirect Resource; Gui: Direct Resource

Take Bing: it is yang Fire, and Wood generates Fire, so it is "I generate it". Jia and Bing are both yang, the same polarity, so Bing is the Eating God. Repeat the steps a few times and you can derive the Ten Gods for any Day Master.

What does each of the five groups describe?

Traditionally the five groups are understood along these lines. They are themes to observe, not verdicts about a person:

  • Friend and Rob Wealth: self, peers, forces on an equal footing.
  • Eating God and Hurting Officer: expression, creativity, the ways ideas are put out into the world.
  • Wealth stars: resources and things one manages and works with.
  • Officer and Seven Killings: rules, responsibility, pressure and outside structure.
  • Resource stars: support, learning, absorbing and being cared for.

"Direct" and "indirect" come from the polarity difference. Traditional explanations often describe the direct form as steadier and more measured and the indirect form as more forthright and changeable, but neither ranks above the other.

How do you read Ten Gods in the branches?

Branches have their own phase, but for the Ten Gods you usually look at their hidden stems. For example, the branch Yin contains Jia, Bing and Wu; compare each with the Day Master to get three Ten Gods. Hidden stems have a main and secondary order; beginners can start with the main one.

What do beginners often misunderstand?

First, reading the names literally, assuming the Hurting Officer means getting hurt or the Seven Killings means something bad. These are traditional names for types of relationship and should be understood through generation, control and polarity.

Second, drawing conclusions from a single Ten God. Each one has to be read in the whole chart: which pillar it sits in, how many there are, and how it interacts with the other characters.

FAQ

Do I have to memorise the Ten Gods?
Not by rote. Remember the five relationships, same as me, I generate, I control, controls me and generates me, plus same or different polarity, and you can derive them yourself.
Why isn't the Day Master itself one of the Ten Gods?
The Ten Gods describe how the other characters relate to the Day Master. The Day Master is the reference point, so it doesn't get a separate name.
Does 'god' mean a deity?
No. 'God' is simply the traditional term for a type of relationship and has nothing to do with religious belief.
