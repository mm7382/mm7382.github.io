The 2027 I Ching Year Guide: Approach and the rhythm of the year

By Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-24

Step closer to opportunity, and grow from steady roots. Read the rhythm of the year through Approach.

This is the full 2027 annual feature from the YiJian app, free to read here and in the app, with no subscription. It is a yearly life guide built around a hexagram, not a personal prediction.

Welcome the opportunity, and keep the foundation

The proposal for 2027 is to draw closer. Closer to work worth deepening, closer to people you can be honest with, and closer to a life you can genuinely carry. Reading the year through Approach, the point is not to wait for good things to arrive, but to be ready to receive people and opportunities when they come to you.

Lin means to approach and to look after. Take a step forward, and also look one step further: take stock of resources before expanding, clarify responsibility before saying yes. Let growth have roots, and let enthusiasm keep its measure.

Hexagram of the year: Approach

Approach places Kun above and Dui below: earth above, lake below. Kun's capacity to carry and Dui's openness to exchange form the two threads of this guide: receive opportunity on a stable base, and build relationships through clear communication.

Read the six lines from the bottom up: the lowest two lines are yang, the four above are yin. From this we take the image of strength growing step by step, a reminder to finish the small things first and scale up afterwards. This is a cultural way of reading symbols, not a way to determine what will happen to any individual.

This piece follows one Huangji Jingshi annual hexagram sequence, using Approach as a shared reference for 2027; other methods of derivation may give different results.

Work: be trusted first, be seen after

Approach, applied to work, means walking to where the problem actually is. Rather than waiting to be assigned, clear up the one thing in front of you that is stuck: fill in the missing information, confirm the deadline, let the people working with you know the next step.

Kun above is a reminder that taking on a task means being able to hold it; Dui below is a reminder to explain what you can do. If you are preparing to change jobs, first gather the work and results that prove your ability; if you lead a team, ask what is blocking people before you raise demands.

  • Worth doing: Review your results once a quarter; put important agreements in writing; run a trial before scaling up.
  • Better avoided: Taking on everything to look committed; treating verbal praise as a promise of promotion; letting busyness stand in for delivery.

Money: give income a destination, give spending a boundary

See Kun as the container and Dui as the flow, and this year's financial practice becomes: when income arrives, decide first how to keep it. Opportunities that look like growth can bring added costs and responsibilities with them.

Separate necessary spending, near-term plans, and the amount you can afford to put at risk. Test a new source of income at small scale first; give yourself time to compare and consider before any large purchase. This guide does not forecast market movements and does not name times to buy or sell.

  • Worth doing: Review income and spending monthly; leave room for uncertain costs; read fees and exit terms before committing.
  • Better avoided: Lending or borrowing out of social pressure; spending income you have not received; taking on more risk because this year looks strong.

Wellbeing: look after the everyday, and leave yourself room

Lin also means to look after. Applied to wellbeing, we take it as a reminder to care for yourself actively; we do not read illness or constitution from a hexagram. Early in the year, notice your sleep, your activity and your stress, and find the one you most easily neglect.

Kun's steadiness can become a more regular daily rhythm; Dui's openness can become a willingness to say you are tired and ask for support. Looking after yourself does not have to start with a large plan: choose one small change you can keep.

  • Worth doing: Protect time for rest; choose daily activity that suits you; seek medical help for discomfort that persists or worries you.
  • Better avoided: Proving yourself by pushing through; explaining symptoms to yourself through a hexagram; delaying care because of what an annual reading says.

Relationships: approach gently, keep boundaries clear

Dui's openness brings people closer, and Kun's acceptance gives the relationship somewhere to rest. Still, being considerate is not the same as always agreeing. Say what you need, and respect the other person's room to say no: that is how a relationship lasts.

  • Worth doing: Listen fully before responding; agree who does what before collaborating; offer specific care to the people who matter to you.
  • Better avoided: Deciding what is best for someone else; treating silence as consent; holding resentment down for a long time to keep the peace.

2027 theme colors: wear the reminder

Warm gold is a reminder to value what you accumulate. Keep it on a small accessory or a notebook cover; when you see it, ask what you finished today.

Ivory white is a reminder to leave space. One less object on the desk, one less reluctant yes in the calendar, so the important things have room.

Deep indigo is a reminder to stay composed. Use it on work items or everyday clothing; when you are being rushed, look at the information clearly before you decide.

These three colors were chosen by YiJian for the 2027 annual feature, standing for accumulation, space and composure. They are design and living reminders, not colors calculated from the bagua, and wearing them does not change your fortune.

Turning trouble around starts with steadying the situation

First, pause. When something changes suddenly, separate what has already happened from what you are worried about, and avoid irreversible decisions while information is thin.

Second, find your footing. List the time, the resources and the trusted people available to you. For anything involving health, contracts or money, let the right professional help.

Third, take a small step. Choose an action you can finish today and adjust if it goes wrong, agree when you will review the result, and only then decide whether to commit more.

Approach reminds us to move with the current while still tending the roots. Turning trouble around is a way of facing difficulty here, not a promise that misfortune disappears.

Make this year something you can keep

Write down three things today: the one thing you most want to do well this year, the one boundary you most need to hold, and the one person you can turn to when things get hard. Come back to them monthly, instead of waiting until the year ends.

May you have the courage to meet opportunity when it comes close, and the room to turn when things change.
