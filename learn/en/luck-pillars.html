What are luck pillars (Da Yun) in Bazi?

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-10-24

A Bazi chart's four pillars are fixed, but traditional reading does not stop at those eight characters. It also uses luck pillars, Da Yun: a sequence of ten-year stem-and-branch periods. This article explains what they are, how they are laid out, where the starting age comes from, and why tools can disagree.

What is a luck pillar?

A luck pillar is one stem-and-branch pair layered on top of the natal four pillars, each covering ten years. The natal chart never changes; what changes is the pair that comes in every ten years and how it relates to the natal chart. The same chart is therefore read as a different combination during different luck pillars.

How are luck pillars laid out?

They are derived from the Month Pillar 月柱, counting through the sixty-cycle either forward or backward:

  1. Look at the polarity of the year stem, then at the person's sex. Yang-year men and yin-year women form one group and count forward through the sixty-cycle; yin-year men and yang-year women form the other and count backward.
  2. Starting from the month pillar, take the next pair in that direction. That is the first luck pillar.
  3. Keep going in the same direction for the second, third and later pillars, ten years each.

If the month pillar is Bing-Yin and the direction is forward, the first luck pillar is Ding-Mao, then Wu-Chen and Ji-Si. Counting backward instead gives Yi-Chou, Jia-Zi and Gui-Hai.

What does the starting age mean?

The first luck pillar does not begin at birth. It begins at a starting age, after which a new pillar comes in every ten years. The starting age is computed from the distance between the moment of birth and the nearest solar term: forward counting looks ahead to the next solar term, backward counting looks back to the previous one, and that interval is converted into years of age.

A common conversion treats three days as one year, with any remainder scaled into months and days. It should be said plainly that these arithmetic conventions vary between schools: how many days stand for a year, how remainders are rounded, and whether the hour is included all differ. Two tools can therefore give starting ages a year or two apart for the same birth, and a chart tool's result depends on which convention it uses.

A pillar is usually split in half

Ten years is one block, but many approaches split a luck pillar into its stem half and its branch half: the first five years weight the stem, the last five weight the branch. This too is a point of difference between schools. Some read the pillar as a whole, and some divide it another way.

How do annual pillars sit on top?

The Fluid Year is the stem and branch of the calendar year itself; 2026, for example, is a Bing-Wu year. It sits on top of the Luck Pillar, giving three layers: the natal chart underneath, the Luck Pillar as the ten-year background, and the Fluid Year as the two extra characters for that year. Readers look at how the Fluid Year relates to the Luck Pillar and the natal chart, and some methods go further still, down to the fluid month and the fluid day.

How do you read a luck pillar?

The point is not whether a pillar is "good", but how it relates:

  • What Ten Gods relationship its stem has to the Day Master (same as me, produced by me, controlled by me, controlling me, producing me, then split by polarity).
  • Whether its branch has one of the familiar interactions with the four natal branches, such as being identical, combining or clashing.
  • Whether it supplies a phase the natal chart has little of, or reinforces one it already has plenty of.

In other words, you need to know your Day Master and the phase distribution of your natal chart before a luck pillar has anything to be compared against. That is why the usual learning order is four pillars first, then the Day Master, and luck pillars last.

One closing note: luck pillars are a framework for laying out a timeline and thinking about your own situation, not a guarantee about the future. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.

FAQ

Do luck pillars change my natal chart?
No. The eight characters of the four pillars are fixed. A luck pillar is a separate stem and branch layered on top, used to look at how the combination shifts over time.
Why do different tools give different starting ages?
Because the starting age is converted from the time between birth and the nearest solar term, and schools use different conversion ratios and rounding. The result depends on which convention the tool follows.
Which matters more, the luck pillar or the annual pillar?
Traditionally they are read together: the luck pillar is the ten-year background and the annual pillar is that year's stem and branch, both read against the Day Master and the natal chart. Schools disagree on how much weight each carries.
