The 2027 year guides: when each of the four systems starts its year

Written by Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-26

Short answer: this site has four 2027 year guides, one for each system, and their "2027" is not the same stretch of time. The Gregorian year starts on 1 January. The Bazi year starts at Li Chun, around 4 February. The lunar year and the zodiac animal start on 6 February. The astrological year starts at the spring equinox, around 20 March.

So before asking what 2027 holds, decide which system you are asking. This page puts the four guides side by side: what each covers, who should read which first, and how the four year boundaries compare.

All four guides are public cultural readings, not personal predictions. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.

Four systems, four year boundaries

The same phrase, "2027", points at four different stretches of time, resting on four different definitions.

SystemStart of 2027Basis
Gregorian calendar1 JanuaryAn administrative convention, unrelated to the sky
Bazi and the Zi Wei annual transformationsLi Chun, around 4 FebruaryThe 24 solar terms; the Sun reaches 315° of ecliptic longitude
Lunar calendar and zodiac animalFirst day of the first lunar month, 6 FebruaryThe synodic month; day one is the day of the new moon
Western astrologySpring equinox, around 20 MarchThe tropical zodiac; the Sun returns to 0° Aries

Two points are often missed. First, Li Chun and lunar new year are not the same day, so between 4 and 6 February the Bazi year is already Ding-Wei while the lunar calendar is still at the tail of Bing-Wu. Second, Li Chun and the equinox are moments, not whole days, and because time zones differ the same moment can fall on the previous or the next date in another country. For hour-level precision you need the astronomical data for that year. The full derivation is in The 2027 Astrology Year Guide.

Which one to read first

  • You are weighing up one concrete thing right now: read the I Ching guide, then cast a hexagram with Yijian (the casting tool and its lessons are in Traditional Chinese, with English static versions of the lessons). The I Ching answers a question about now, not a whole year.
  • You want the emphasis of the whole year: read the Zi Wei guide. It turns the annual transformations into reminders for work, money, relationships and wellbeing — the closest thing here to what most people mean by an annual forecast.
  • You want to know which of the five phases is heavy for you: read the Bazi guide, then chart your own four pillars with Shilai – Bazi.
  • You want to understand how the sky itself is calculated: read the astrology guide. It gives no predictions — it gives the structure and a method for looking things up yourself.
  • You only want one thing to do this month: skip the year guides and read the Zi Wei Monthly Guide.

Three things not to misread

  • The four systems do not verify each other. Their inputs, bases and scope all differ. Two guides saying different things does not mean one is wrong.
  • A year guide does not predict events. None of the four has your birth data; they describe a year's structure, not what will happen to you.
  • Different year boundaries are not errors. The Gregorian calendar, the solar terms, the synodic month and the tropical zodiac each have their own definition. What matters is knowing which one you are reading.
