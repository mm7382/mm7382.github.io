The fourteen major stars of Zi Wei Dou Shu: a reference table

By Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-19

This page is a star-by-star reference for the fourteen major stars of Zi Wei Dou Shu, written for readers who already know what a major star is and want the next level of detail. Every entry gives the same five fields: its essence, the two sides of the common reading, how it is read across the twelve palaces, its star group and related stars, and a common misreading.

Three things first. The entries below are this site's own synthesis of the inherited interpretive tradition, not quotations from a classical text. The tradition has no simply auspicious or inauspicious stars, which is why every entry carries two sides. And this site introduces the whole system as a language for reflection and self-observation, with no claim that it predicts events; decide medical, legal and financial matters on ordinary practical grounds and professional advice.

If you do not yet know what a major star is, start with What are the major stars in Zi Wei Dou Shu?

How to use this page

Reading a palace usually begins with its major star. Find which one or two major stars sit in a palace on your own chart, then look those up here. Do not apply the palace column as a conclusion: an actual reading also weighs the other stars sharing the palace, the facing and triangle palaces, the four transformations and the brightness levels.

The Zi Wei group: six stars

Zi Wei (Purple Emperor) 紫微

Essence
The emperor star, standing for a sense of position, authority and taking charge.
Common reading
Commonly read as self-directed, face-conscious and willing to carry responsibility; the other side is stubbornness, difficulty backing down, and a need for recognition.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as self-positioning and a tendency to lead; in the Career Palace as managing or owning responsibility; in the Spouse Palace as leading within the relationship and needing respect; in the Wealth Palace as a sense of control over resources.
Star group and related stars
Head of the Zi Wei group. Tradition looks at whether Zuo Fu, You Bi, Tian Kui and Tian Yue sit with it or face it, to discuss whether support is present.
A common misreading
The most common misreading is that Zi Wei in the Life Palace means a good life. It is a set of images, and the tradition equally stresses its difficulty when it stands without support. It is not a verdict.

Tian Ji (Heavenly Mechanism) 天機

Essence
Movement and change; the image of thinking, planning and shifting position.
Common reading
Commonly read as quick-thinking, analytical and fond of planning; the other side is overthinking, changeability and difficulty settling.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as a way of thinking and a tendency to change; in the Travel Palace as movement and changing surroundings; in the Siblings Palace as calculation and coordination in dealings; in the Career Palace as work that demands thinking or shifts often.
Star group and related stars
Zi Wei group. Tradition often reads it together with Tai Yin, Tian Liang and Ju Men, in groupings such as Ji-Yue-Tong-Liang.
A common misreading
Often flattened into a cleverness star. In the tradition it also carries instability and toil; taking only the clever side gives a skewed reading.

Tai Yang (Sun) 太陽

Essence
Shining and giving out; the image of outward expression and provision.
Common reading
Commonly read as warm, generous and willing to look after others; the other side is over-giving, tending to outsiders while neglecting oneself, and tiring easily.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as how one shows up outwardly; in the Parents Palace, relations with male elders are a traditional entry point; in the Career Palace, work that is public-facing; in the Fortune Palace, the spending of one's energy.
Star group and related stars
Zi Wei group. Tradition reads it against Tai Yin as day against night, and uses the earthly branch of its palace to discuss brightness levels.
A common misreading
Often stated as Tai Yang meaning the father. That is one traditional image among several, not the only reading, and nothing about a real family can be inferred from it.

Wu Qu (Military Melody) 武曲

Essence
Execution and hardness; the image of doing, deciding and handling money.
Common reading
Commonly read as decisive, practical and action-first; the other side is bluntness, little room for manoeuvre and a hard edge.
Across the twelve palaces
The Wealth Palace is where tradition discusses it most, as an attitude to and handling of money; in the Life Palace as a style of action; in the Spouse Palace as a direct way of expressing oneself.
Star group and related stars
Zi Wei group. Tradition often reads it with Tian Fu and Tan Lang, and checks whether it meets the four transformations.
A common misreading
Often equated with wealth itself. The tradition speaks of how money is handled and regarded, not of amounts.

Tian Tong (Heavenly Sameness) 天同

Essence
Harmony and ease; the image of seeking stability and smoothing conflict.
Common reading
Commonly read as easy-going, agreeable and able to enjoy life; the other side is inertia, avoiding problems and lacking drive.
Across the twelve palaces
The Fortune Palace is a traditional focus, read as state of mind and enjoyment; in the Life Palace as softness in dealing with things; in the Health Palace tradition discusses routine and care.
Star group and related stars
Zi Wei group. Tradition reads it with Tian Ji, Tian Liang and Tai Yin, often in the Ji-Yue-Tong-Liang discussion.
A common misreading
Often read as laziness. The traditional image is sameness, centred on harmonising and not contending, which is not the same thing.

Lian Zhen (Chastity) 廉貞

Essence
Principle alongside desire; the image of holding a line and of entanglement.
Common reading
Commonly read as principled, particular and inwardly intense; the other side is going in circles, emotional swings and friction with others.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as inner pull; in the Career Palace as insistence on professional standards; in the Spouse Palace tradition discusses intensity and friction in the relationship.
Star group and related stars
Zi Wei group. Tradition pays particular attention to whether it takes the Hua Ji transformation, and to its relation with Tan Lang, Qi Sha and Po Jun.
A common misreading
The name contains a word for chastity, which misleads. In the tradition it is a secondary romance star with complex imagery; the name should not be read literally.

The Tian Fu group: eight stars

Tian Fu (Heavenly Treasury) 天府

Essence
Storehouse and consolidation; the image of accumulating, safekeeping and steadiness.
Common reading
Commonly read as steady, dependable and good at holding ground; the other side is conservatism, reluctance to risk and slow change.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as a leaning towards stability; in the Wealth Palace as ways of accumulating and safekeeping; the Property Palace is a traditional focus, read as the base of stability.
Star group and related stars
Head of the Tian Fu group. Tradition reads it against Zi Wei; the two head their own groups and their positions interlock when the chart is cast.
A common misreading
Often said to mean having a treasury. The tradition speaks of a guarding, storing quality, not of actual assets.

Tai Yin (Moon) 太陰

Essence
Inwardness and nurture; the image of feeling, saving and caring.
Common reading
Commonly read as fine-grained, considerate and attentive to feeling; the other side is sensitivity, worry and held-in emotion.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as inner feeling; in the Property and Wealth Palaces tradition discusses savings and dwelling; in the Parents Palace, relations with female elders are a common entry point.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. Paired against Tai Yang, and tradition also reads brightness from the earthly branch of its palace.
A common misreading
Often equated with the mother or with women. That is one traditional image; nothing about gender or family facts can be inferred from it.

Tan Lang (Greedy Wolf) 貪狼

Essence
Desire and versatility; the image of pursuit, sociability and breadth of learning.
Common reading
Commonly read as wide-interested, socially capable and persuasive; the other side is wanting too much, difficulty focusing and distraction.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as the direction of desire; in the Spouse Palace tradition discusses attraction and interaction; in the Career Palace, work that needs sociability or a craft.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. The principal romance star in the tradition, often read with Lian Zhen, with particular attention to Hua Lu and Hua Ji.
A common misreading
The name contains greed, so it is often taken as negative. In the tradition it also stands for craft and learning; it is not a bad star.

Ju Men (Great Gate) 巨門

Essence
Speech and obstruction; the image of speaking, questioning and distinguishing, and also of barriers.
Common reading
Commonly read as articulate, probing and discerning; the other side is suspicion, disputes over words and easy misunderstanding.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as a leaning towards expression and doubt; in the Siblings and Spouse Palaces tradition discusses friction in communication; in the Career Palace, work that relies on speaking or analysis.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. Its position relative to Tai Yang is a common discussion point, and it is often read with Tian Ji.
A common misreading
Often called a star of strife and therefore bad. The tradition also treats it as the capacity to discern and seek truth; both sides belong together.

Tian Xiang (Chancellor) 天相

Essence
Assisting and decorum; the image of helping, mediating and a sense of form.
Common reading
Commonly read as loyal, rule-keeping and good at coordinating; the other side is depending on circumstances, lacking one's own stance and being easily swayed.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as how one cooperates; in the Career Palace as an assisting or mediating role; in the Spouse Palace tradition discusses accommodation.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. Tradition pays particular attention to the stars in the palaces on either side of it, in the sandwiching patterns.
A common misreading
Often dismissed as a star without character. What the tradition emphasises is exactly how much its surroundings shape it; that is its trait, not a flaw.

Tian Liang (Heavenly Beam) 天梁

Essence
Shelter and responsibility; the image of looking after, principle and a sense of seniority.
Common reading
Commonly read as having an elder's bearing, willing to shoulder things and principled; the other side is lecturing, stubbornness and keeping distance.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as shouldering and principle; in the Parents and Health Palaces tradition discusses shelter and care; in the Career Palace, roles that gatekeep or supervise.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. Read with Tian Tong, Tian Ji and Tai Yin, and part of the Ji-Yue-Tong-Liang discussion.
A common misreading
Often said to mean a protector will appear so nothing goes wrong. The traditional image presumes trouble first and shelter after; it does not mean trouble is avoided.

Qi Sha (Seven Killings) 七殺

Essence
Initiating and cutting off; the image of charging ahead, independence and cutting losses.
Common reading
Commonly read as bold, independent and quick to act; the other side is impatience, leaving no margin and carrying things alone.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as drive in action; in the Travel Palace tradition discusses leaving and opening new ground; in the Career Palace, pioneering or high-pressure work.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. With Po Jun and Tan Lang it forms the Sha-Po-Lang trio, whose positions always face one another and which is a standard analytic grouping.
A common misreading
The name contains killing, so it is often taken as a malefic. In the tradition it stands for initiating; the reading is about the intensity of change, not good or bad.

Po Jun (Army Breaker) 破軍

Essence
Breaking and rebuilding; the image of dispersing what exists and starting again.
Common reading
Commonly read as willing to change, unbound by frames and able to start over; the other side is repetition, high cost and difficulty accumulating.
Across the twelve palaces
In the Life Palace it is read as the scale of change; in the Property and Wealth Palaces tradition discusses gathering and scattering; in the Career Palace, work that restructures or pivots.
Star group and related stars
Tian Fu group. One of the Sha-Po-Lang trio, with particular attention to whether it takes Hua Lu or Hua Quan.
A common misreading
Often said to mean losing money. The traditional image is breaking before building; it describes a mode of change, not an outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Which are the fourteen major stars?
Six in the Zi Wei group: Zi Wei, Tian Ji, Tai Yang, Wu Qu, Tian Tong, Lian Zhen. Eight in the Tian Fu group: Tian Fu, Tai Yin, Tan Lang, Ju Men, Tian Xiang, Tian Liang, Qi Sha, Po Jun.
Where does the content of this table come from?
The entries are this site's own synthesis of the inherited interpretive tradition of Zi Wei Dou Shu. The site introduces that tradition as a language for reflection and self-observation, with no claim that it predicts events.
Why does every star list two sides?
The tradition has no simply auspicious or inauspicious stars. The same star reads differently in different palaces and with different companions, so taking only one side gives a skewed reading.
Can the palace column be applied directly?
No. That column is only a broad image. An actual reading also considers the stars sharing the palace, the facing and triangle palaces, the four transformations and the brightness levels; no judgement should rest on one star alone.
