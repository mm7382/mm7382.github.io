The 2027 Bazi Year Guide: how to read the Ding-Wei year

By Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-24

Short answer: the 2027 annual pillar in Bazi is Ding Wei. The stem is Ding (yin Fire), the branch is Wei (yin Earth). It is number 44 of the sixty-year cycle, its na yin element is Heavenly River Water, and its zodiac animal is the Goat. The year pillar changes at Lichun, around 4 February 2027 — not at Lunar New Year.

This page does not predict what will happen to you. It teaches something more useful: once you know the year is Ding Wei, how do you actually read it against your own chart?

This is an explanation of traditional Bazi method, not a personal forecast. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.

What Ding Wei is, one character at a time

An annual pillar is just one stem plus one branch. Ding Wei has three layers worth knowing.

The stem Ding: yin Fire. Of the ten stems, Bing is yang Fire and Ding is yin Fire. The classical image is that Bing is like the sun and Ding is like a lamp or candle flame: the same element, but one is broad heat and the other is small and sustained. It is a memory image for polarity, not a physical claim.

The branch Wei: yin Earth. Wei is the eighth of the twelve branches. It maps to the Goat, and to the sixth lunar month — the last month of summer.

The stems hidden in Wei: Ji, Ding and Yi. Every branch hides one to three stems. Wei hides Ji (Earth, its main stem), Ding (Fire) and Yi (Wood). So Wei is never read as plain Earth: it carries late-summer Fire and it stores Wood. Because Yi Wood is stored here, Wei is called the storehouse of Wood.

Put the two together and the stem produces the branch: Fire generates Earth, so Ding sits on Wei without clashing with it.

When the year pillar changes: Lichun, not New Year

This is asked every year, and got wrong every year.

Bazi divides years and months by the twenty-four solar terms, and the year pillar changes at Lichun. Lichun 2027 falls around 4 February (check the exact time for the year), so a birthday in January 2027 still has Bing Wu as its year pillar; Ding Wei starts after Lichun.

Three things to watch:

  • Lunar New Year 2027 is 6 February, about two days after Lichun. The zodiac animal is popularly counted from New Year, the Bazi year pillar from Lichun, so the two can disagree.
  • Some lineages do change the year at Lunar New Year. If your birthday falls in that gap, two tools can give two different year pillars. That is a difference of tradition, not a bug.
  • Lichun has an hour and a minute, not just a date. If you were born close to the boundary, you need the time too.

Four relationships between Wei and the branches in your chart

An annual branch is never read alone — you compare it with the four branches already in your chart. These are fixed traditional rules, not guesses.

  • Six combination: Wu and Wei combine. If your chart has Wu, the annual Wei forms a combination with it.
  • Six clash: Chou clashes Wei. Chou sits opposite Wei, so a Chou in your chart makes a clash.
  • Three harmony: Hai, Mao and Wei form the Wood frame. If your chart already has Hai and Mao, the annual Wei completes it.
  • Three punishment: Chou, Xu and Wei. All three are needed for the full punishment; readings for only two of them differ between lineages.

Combinations, clashes and punishments describe structure. They are not good or bad in themselves. As a beginner, first learn to spot whether one is present, then study how different lineages read it.

The void branches for Ding Wei: Yin and Mao

The sixty pillars fall into six decades of ten. Ten pillars use up ten branches, and the two of the twelve that never get a stem in that decade are the void branches (kong wang).

Ding Wei belongs to the Jia Chen decade: Jia Chen, Yi Si, Bing Wu, Ding Wei, Wu Shen, Ji You, Geng Xu, Xin Hai, Ren Zi, Gui Chou. Those use Chen, Si, Wu, Wei, Shen, You, Xu, Hai, Zi and Chou — which leaves Yin and Mao as the void branches.

Ten Gods table: what Ding Wei is for your Day Master

The Ten Gods are relationships measured from the Day Master, the stem of your day pillar. Once you know your Day Master you can read Ding, and the main stem of Wei (Ji Earth), straight off the table. The derivation is fixed: same phase as me is Peer or Rival, what I produce is Eating God or Hurting Officer, what I control is Indirect or Direct Wealth, what controls me is Seven Killings or Direct Officer, what produces me is Indirect or Direct Resource — the first name when the polarity matches, the second when it differs.

Four steps to read the year against your own chart

  1. Find your Day Master. The stem of the day pillar is the reference point for everything else.
  2. Look up the annual Ten Gods. Use the table above for Ding and for Wei.
  3. Compare the branches. Check Wei against each of your four branches for the Wu–Wei combination, the Chou–Wei clash, the Hai–Mao–Wei frame or the Chou–Xu–Wei punishment.
  4. Add the luck pillar. A year lasts one year; a luck pillar lasts ten. The same year reads differently inside different luck pillars — see What are luck pillars (Da Yun)?

To go further, Is my Day Master strong or weak? explains why one annual pillar means different things in different charts.

Three things not to misread

  • An annual pillar does not decide events. Stems and branches describe structure and relationship, not outcomes. Any claim that a year makes something inevitable goes beyond what Bazi can say.
  • There is no one-size-fits-all year reading in Bazi. The method starts from your own four pillars, and the same Ding Wei year forms ten different relationships across the ten Day Masters.
  • Differing lineages are not errors. The year boundary, punishment readings and strength assessment genuinely differ between traditions. What matters while learning is knowing which one you are using.
