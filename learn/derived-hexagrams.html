錯卦、綜卦、互卦是什麼？一個卦能推出的四種衍生卦

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-27

起卦得到一個卦之後，古書常不只讀這一個卦，而是從同一個卦再推出別的卦來對照。最常見的有四種：之卦、錯卦、綜卦、互卦。它們的求法都是固定的畫法，不需要再擲一次。這一頁把四種的做法、例子與限制一次講清楚。

先說明：底下是本站對流傳解讀傳統的整理，名稱在歷代注家中用法略有出入，本站採用最通行的一組。「錯」「綜」這兩個字的用法常被歸到明代來知德的《周易集註》。這套語言在本站被當成反思與自我觀察的工具介紹，不主張它能預測事件。

四種衍生卦一覽

六爻由下往上依序叫初爻、二爻、三爻、四爻、五爻、上爻。四種衍生卦的差別就在「動哪幾爻」：

名稱別名怎麼求由什麼決定
之卦變卦只把這一次的動爻陰陽翻轉這一次擲出的數字
錯卦旁通卦、對卦六爻陰陽全部互換本卦的形狀
綜卦反卦、覆卦把整個卦上下顛倒本卦的形狀
互卦互體、中爻取二三四爻為下卦、三四五爻為上卦本卦的形狀

這是最關鍵的一條分界：只有之卦跟這一次起卦有關。錯卦、綜卦、互卦完全由本卦的形狀決定，所以同一個卦的錯、綜、互永遠是同一個答案，查表就能得到。

錯卦：六爻陰陽全部互換

把本卦的每一爻都換成相反的：陽爻改陰爻、陰爻改陽爻，六爻都換，得到的就是錯卦。

  • 乾卦六爻全陽，全部換成陰，錯卦是坤卦。
  • 水雷屯（第 3 卦）的錯卦是火風鼎（第 50 卦）。
  • 水天需（第 5 卦）的錯卦是火地晉（第 35 卦）。

錯卦一定是另一個卦，不可能是自己——六爻全換之後不會回到原來的樣子。所以六十四卦剛好兩兩配成三十二組錯卦。傳統上把錯卦當成「完全相反的處境」：同一件事換成對立的立場來看會是什麼樣子。

綜卦：把整個卦上下顛倒

把本卦整個翻過來，原本的初爻變成上爻、二爻變成五爻，以此類推，得到的就是綜卦。

  • 水雷屯（第 3 卦）顛倒過來是山水蒙（第 4 卦）。
  • 水天需（第 5 卦）顛倒過來是天水訟（第 6 卦）。

這就是為什麼六十四卦的通行卦序大多兩兩相鄰：屯與蒙、需與訟，都是一組綜卦。

八個顛倒後還是自己的卦

有八個卦的陰陽排列本身上下對稱，顛倒之後和原來一模一樣，綜卦就是自己：

乾、坤、坎、離、頤、大過、中孚、小過。

這八個卦沒辦法用綜卦配對，所以卦序改用陰陽全換來配：乾配坤、坎配離、頤配大過、中孚配小過。這四組同時也是錯卦。

互卦：取中間四爻重新疊成一卦

互卦不用全部六爻，只取中間四爻，而且中間兩爻要用兩次：

  • 第二、三、四爻由下往上排，當成互卦的下卦。
  • 第三、四、五爻由下往上排，當成互卦的上卦。

初爻與上爻被捨去。以水雷屯（第 3 卦）為例，六爻由下往上是陽、陰、陰、陰、陽、陰：

  • 二三四爻是陰、陰、陰，下卦為坤 ☷。
  • 三四五爻是陰、陰、陽，上卦為艮 ☶。
  • 上艮下坤，互卦是山地剝（第 23 卦）。

同樣算法：山水蒙（第 4 卦）的互卦是地雷復（第 24 卦），水天需（第 5 卦）的互卦是火澤睽（第 38 卦），天水訟（第 6 卦）的互卦是風火家人（第 37 卦）。

互卦只有十六種

因為初爻與上爻被丟掉，很多不同的卦會得到同一個互卦。把六十四卦逐卦推一遍，答案只會落在十六個卦上：

乾、坤、剝、復、頤、大過、家人、睽、蹇、解、夬、姤、漸、歸妹、既濟、未濟。

其中只有乾與坤的互卦是自己——乾的中間四爻全陽，推出來還是乾；坤同理。傳統上把互卦當成「藏在裡面的狀況」：表面的卦講處境，互卦講這個處境內部正在發生什麼。

四個特別的卦：錯卦和綜卦是同一個

大多數卦的錯卦與綜卦是兩個不同的卦，但有四個卦例外：

  • 地天泰（第 11 卦）與天地否（第 12 卦）互為錯卦，也互為綜卦。
  • 水火既濟（第 63 卦）與火水未濟（第 64 卦）同樣如此。

原因是它們的陰陽排列同時滿足兩種對稱：全部互換和上下顛倒都會得到同一個結果。這也是「否極泰來」這句話在結構上特別工整的原因。

怎麼用：角度，不是答案

四種衍生卦提供四個不同的觀看角度，不會加總成一個分數，也沒有哪一個比較準：

  • 本卦：現在的處境。
  • 之卦：這次的變化要往哪裡去。
  • 錯卦：完全相反的立場長什麼樣。
  • 綜卦：換成對方的位置來看同一件事。
  • 互卦：這個處境內部藏著什麼。

初學者不必每次都算四種。先把本卦與動爻讀熟，想多一個對照角度時再取一種。想先補起卦與動爻的基礎，見動爻、本卦與之卦是什麼？；想看六十四卦兩兩配對的完整說明，見六十四卦是什麼？卦名、上下卦與卦序一次看懂；不會查卦名時，見六十四卦怎麼查？用上下卦找到卦名與卦序。

常見問題

錯卦、綜卦、互卦和之卦差在哪？
之卦只翻轉這一次擲出的動爻，所以同一個本卦可能有不同的之卦。錯卦、綜卦、互卦不看動爻，純粹由本卦的形狀決定，所以一個卦的錯卦、綜卦、互卦永遠固定。
哪些卦上下顛倒後還是自己？
八個：乾、坤、坎、離、頤、大過、中孚、小過。它們的綜卦就是自己，所以六十四卦排序時改用陰陽全換來配對：乾配坤、坎配離、頤配大過、中孚配小過。
互卦為什麼只有十六種？
互卦只取本卦的第二到第五爻重新組合，初爻與上爻被捨去，不同的卦常常得到同一個互卦。把六十四卦逐卦推一遍，結果只會落在十六個卦上：乾、坤、剝、復、頤、大過、家人、睽、蹇、解、夬、姤、漸、歸妹、既濟、未濟。
有沒有卦的錯卦和綜卦是同一個？
有四個卦會這樣：泰與否互為錯卦，也互為綜卦；既濟與未濟同樣如此。因為它們的陰陽排列同時滿足「全部互換」和「上下顛倒」兩種對稱。
解卦一定要把四種都算出來嗎？
不用。衍生卦是提供不同的觀察角度，不是必要步驟，也不會加總成一個分數。初學者先把本卦與動爻讀熟，之後想多一個對照角度時再取一種來看就夠了。

What are the opposite, inverted and nuclear hexagrams?

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-27

Once you have cast a hexagram, the classical commentaries rarely stop at that one figure. They derive further hexagrams from the same cast and read them side by side. Four are common: the resulting, opposite, inverted and nuclear hexagrams. Each is produced by a fixed drawing rule, with no second cast. This page sets out how to derive each one, with worked examples and their limits.

A note first: this is our summary of a received interpretive tradition. The names are used slightly differently by different commentators, and we follow the most common set; the terms rendered here as "opposite" and "inverted" are usually traced to Lai Zhide's Ming-dynasty commentary. On this site the I Ching is presented as a tool for reflection and self-observation, not as a way to predict events.

The four derived hexagrams at a glance

The six lines are counted from the bottom up: first, second, third, fourth, fifth and top. What separates the four derivations is simply which lines move.

NameAlso calledHow to derive itDepends on
Resulting hexagramchanged, relating hexagramFlip only the moving lines of this castthe numbers you cast
Opposite hexagraminverse, lateral-linking hexagramSwap yin and yang in all six linesthe shape of the primary hexagram
Inverted hexagramreversed, overturned hexagramTurn the whole figure upside downthe shape of the primary hexagram
Nuclear hexagraminner, central-lines hexagramLines 2-3-4 as the lower trigram, 3-4-5 as the upperthe shape of the primary hexagram

That last column holds the key distinction: only the resulting hexagram depends on this particular cast. The opposite, inverted and nuclear hexagrams follow from the shape of the primary hexagram alone, so each hexagram always has the same three, and they can simply be looked up.

The opposite hexagram: swap yin and yang in all six lines

Change every line to its contrary, yang to yin and yin to yang, all six of them.

  • Qian is six yang lines; swap them all and you get Kun.
  • The opposite of Zhun (3) is Ding (50).
  • The opposite of Xu (5) is Jin (35).

An opposite hexagram is always a different hexagram, never the same one, because swapping all six lines cannot return you to where you started. So the sixty-four hexagrams fall into exactly thirty-two opposite pairs. The traditional reading treats the opposite as the wholly contrary situation: what this same matter looks like from the other side.

The inverted hexagram: turn the figure upside down

Flip the whole hexagram over, so the bottom line becomes the top line, the second becomes the fifth, and so on.

  • Zhun (3) turned upside down is Meng (4).
  • Xu (5) turned upside down is Song (6).

This is why most hexagrams in the received sequence sit next to their inverse: Zhun with Meng, Xu with Song.

The eight hexagrams that invert into themselves

Eight hexagrams are symmetric top to bottom, so turning them over changes nothing and their inverted hexagram is themselves:

Qian, Kun, Kan, Li, Yi (27), Da Guo (28), Zhong Fu (61) and Xiao Guo (62).

Because these eight cannot be paired by inversion, the sequence pairs them by swapping yin and yang instead: Qian with Kun, Kan with Li, Yi with Da Guo, Zhong Fu with Xiao Guo. Those four pairs are therefore opposite pairs as well.

The nuclear hexagram: rebuild from the middle four lines

The nuclear hexagram uses only the middle four lines, and the two central ones are used twice:

  • Lines two, three and four, bottom up, become the lower trigram.
  • Lines three, four and five, bottom up, become the upper trigram.

The bottom and top lines are discarded. Take Zhun (3), whose lines from the bottom up are yang, yin, yin, yin, yang, yin:

  • Lines two to four are yin, yin, yin, giving Kun as the lower trigram.
  • Lines three to five are yin, yin, yang, giving Gen as the upper trigram.
  • Gen over Kun is Bo (23), so that is the nuclear hexagram.

The same method gives Fu (24) for Meng (4), Kui (38) for Xu (5) and Jia Ren (37) for Song (6).

There are only sixteen nuclear hexagrams

Because the bottom and top lines are thrown away, many different hexagrams share one nuclear hexagram. Work through all sixty-four and the results land on just sixteen figures:

Qian, Kun, Bo (23), Fu (24), Yi (27), Da Guo (28), Jia Ren (37), Kui (38), Jian (39), Xie (40), Gui (43), Gou (44), Jian (53), Gui Mei (54), Ji Ji (63) and Wei Ji (64).

Only Qian and Kun are their own nuclear hexagram: Qian's middle four lines are all yang, so the rebuild returns Qian, and the same holds for Kun. Traditionally the nuclear hexagram is read as the situation hidden inside: the surface figure names the circumstance, and the nuclear hexagram says what is going on within it.

Four hexagrams whose opposite is also their inverse

For most hexagrams the opposite and the inverted hexagram are two different figures. Four are exceptions:

  • Tai (11) and Pi (12) are each other's opposite and also each other's inverse.
  • Ji Ji (63) and Wei Ji (64) behave the same way.

Their line patterns satisfy both symmetries at once: swapping all six lines and turning the figure over lead to the same result. It is also why the saying that Pi at its extreme turns into Tai is so neat structurally.

How to use them: angles, not answers

The four derivations give four ways of looking. They do not add up to a score, and none of them is more accurate than the others.

  • Primary hexagram: how things stand now.
  • Resulting hexagram: where this change is heading.
  • Opposite hexagram: what the wholly contrary position looks like.
  • Inverted hexagram: the same matter seen from the other party's place.
  • Nuclear hexagram: what is hidden inside the situation.

Beginners do not need all four every time. Read the primary hexagram and its moving lines first, then take one derived hexagram when you want a second angle. 常見問題

How do the opposite, inverted and nuclear hexagrams differ from the resulting hexagram?
The resulting hexagram flips only the lines that came out as moving lines in this particular cast, so the same primary hexagram can lead to different resulting hexagrams. The opposite, inverted and nuclear hexagrams ignore the moving lines entirely and depend only on the shape of the primary hexagram, so they never change.
Which hexagrams stay the same when turned upside down?
Eight of them: Qian, Kun, Kan, Li, Yi (27), Da Guo (28), Zhong Fu (61) and Xiao Guo (62). Their inverted hexagram is themselves, so the traditional sequence pairs them by swapping yin and yang instead: Qian with Kun, Kan with Li, Yi with Da Guo, Zhong Fu with Xiao Guo.
Why are there only sixteen nuclear hexagrams?
The nuclear hexagram uses only lines two to five of the original, discarding the bottom and top lines, so many different hexagrams produce the same nuclear hexagram. Work through all sixty-four and the results land on just sixteen: Qian, Kun, Bo (23), Fu (24), Yi (27), Da Guo (28), Jia Ren (37), Kui (38), Jian (39), Xie (40), Gui (43), Gou (44), Jian (53), Gui Mei (54), Ji Ji (63) and Wei Ji (64).
Is any hexagram's opposite also its inverse?
Four hexagrams work that way. Tai (11) and Pi (12) are each other's opposite and also each other's inverse, and the same is true of Ji Ji (63) and Wei Ji (64), because their line patterns satisfy both kinds of symmetry at once.
Do I have to work out all four for every reading?
No. Derived hexagrams offer extra angles; they are not a required step and they do not add up to a score. Learn to read the primary hexagram and its moving lines first, then pick one derived hexagram when you want a second point of view.
