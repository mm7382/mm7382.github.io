線上起卦準嗎？免費線上易經占卜的正確做法

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-13

線上起卦可以，而且和銅錢起卦得到的是同一套六爻結構。差別不在器具是銅錢還是程式，而在三件你自己控制得到的事：問題有沒有寫清楚、有沒有一次擲完就定案、以及工具有沒有把六爻、動爻、本卦與之卦完整顯示出來。這篇說明為什麼程式亂數也算一次起卦、線上與實體的三個真實差別、一次合格的線上起卦怎麼做，以及怎麼判斷一個免費工具夠不夠用。

為什麼程式隨機也算一次起卦？

先看銅錢起卦在做什麼：丟三枚銅錢六次，把正反面換算成 6、7、8、9 四種數字，得到六個數字，也就是六爻。這個做法的核心是「用一個你無法控制的過程取得六個數字」。銅錢用的是手勢、桌面、空氣這些你算不出來的物理因素；線上工具用的是程式亂數。兩者的共同點是：結果不是你挑的。

所以器具材質不是重點。要老實說的是，這是一個立場，不是所有人都同意：有人認為實物擲卦時那段慢下來的、需要專注的過程本身就是方法的一部分，換成按一下就少了那一塊。這個說法有它的道理，但它講的是「人的狀態」，不是「卦的真偽」。這兩件事值得分開看。

線上與銅錢的三個真實差別

  1. 隨機來源不同：一邊是物理上的不確定，一邊是程式亂數。兩者都不是你能指定結果的，但來源確實不同。
  2. 專注程度不同：銅錢要找地方、要擲六次，慢；線上一秒就有結果，快到很容易亂擲。這是線上起卦最大的風險，不是亂數。
  3. 紀錄方式不同：線上工具通常能把結果留下來；用銅錢就得自己抄六個數字。想事後回頭對照，線上反而佔優勢。

一次合格的線上起卦：五個步驟

  1. 先把問題寫成一句話。開放式、一次只問一件、落在你能行動的範圍。寫法見問卦前的五個原則。
  2. 挑一個會顯示完整六爻的工具。只給你一句結論的工具，等於把要讀的東西收走了。
  3. 一次擲完六爻，不重擲。按下去之前先決定：這一次就是這一次。
  4. 把六個數字抄下來，從初爻（最下面）到上爻（最上面）。這一步讓你之後能自己查、自己核對，不必依賴那個工具。
  5. 照順序讀：先讀本卦卦辭，再依動爻數目決定主讀哪一段，最後看之卦的方向。規則見讀卦的六個步驟與動爻、本卦與之卦。

怎麼判斷一個免費線上工具夠不夠用

免費與付費差的通常是解讀的包裝，不是卦本身。五項檢查，前三項要有、後兩項要沒有：

  1. 有顯示六爻各自的陰陽，以及哪幾爻是動爻。
  2. 有同時給本卦與之卦，不是只給一個卦名。
  3. 有附上卦辭與爻辭的原文，讓你能自己讀、自己查出處。
  4. 不強迫登入、不先要 email 才給結果。
  5. 不把結果講成必然、不給你一個吉凶分數或百分比。

線上起卦最常見的四個坑

  • 重擲到喜歡的卦：按鈕太好按。一事一卦、一次定案。
  • 同一題問很多次又問很多工具：結果不同是必然的，因為每次都是獨立的隨機過程，不代表哪一個工具壞了。
  • 只看卦名就下結論：卦名是標籤，爻辭才是內容。
  • 把「吉」「凶」當判決：那是古文裡對處境走向的描述，不是對你人生的宣判。

一句老實話

線上起卦最大的好處是門檻低，最大的風險也是門檻低。真正讓一次起卦有用的，是你願不願意把問題寫清楚、願不願意守住一次定案、願不願意把爻辭讀完。易經不會預知結果，也不保證任何事情發生。醫療、法律、投資這類重大決定，請找合格的專業人士。

常見問題

線上起卦準嗎？一定要用真的銅錢嗎？
銅錢起卦的核心是用一個你無法控制的過程取得六個數字，銅錢靠的是物理上的不確定，線上工具靠的是程式亂數，兩者都不是你挑的。所以不必為了器具材質放棄線上起卦。要留意的是另外三件事：問題有沒有寫清楚、有沒有一次擲完不重擲、工具有沒有完整顯示六爻與之卦。
線上起卦可以重擲嗎？
不建議。線上起卦按一下就有結果，重擲的誘惑比銅錢大得多，但一題擲到出現喜歡的卦，等於是你自己選了答案。傳統做法是一事一卦、一次定案。真的覺得問題問錯了，就把問題改寫清楚，當成另一個問題重新擲一次，並且把前一次的結果一起留著對照。
免費的線上易經工具會不會比較不準？
免費與付費差的通常不是卦，而是解讀的包裝。判斷一個工具夠不夠用，看五件事：有沒有顯示六爻的陰陽與哪幾爻是動爻、有沒有同時給本卦與之卦、有沒有附上卦辭與爻辭原文、會不會強迫登入或留 email、以及會不會把結果講成必然或給你一個分數。前三項有、後兩項沒有，就夠用了。
為什麼同一個問題在不同工具會得到不同卦？
因為每一次起卦都是一次獨立的隨機過程，本來就不會重現。這不是某個工具壞了。想比較的話，比的應該是它有沒有把六爻、動爻、本卦、之卦與原文給齊，而不是它給了哪一個卦。

Free online I Ching reading: how to do one properly

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-13

An online casting is fine, and it gives you exactly the same six-line structure as casting with coins. The difference is not the instrument but three things you control: whether the question is clearly written, whether you cast once and keep the result, and whether the tool shows you all six lines, the changing lines, the primary hexagram and the resulting hexagram. This article explains why a random number still counts as a casting, the three real differences from coins, the five steps of a sound online casting, and how to tell whether a free tool is good enough.

Why does a random number still count as a casting?

Look at what coin casting actually does: you throw three coins six times, convert heads and tails into the numbers 6, 7, 8 and 9, and end up with six numbers — the six lines. The heart of the method is taking six numbers out of a process you cannot steer. Coins rely on your hand, the table and the air, none of which you can compute; an online tool relies on a random number generator. What they share is that the result is not chosen by you.

So the material of the instrument is not the point. To be honest about it, this is a position and not everyone agrees: some hold that the slow, attentive handling of physical coins is part of the method, and that a single click removes that part. There is something to that — but it is a claim about your own state of mind, not about whether the hexagram is genuine. The two are worth keeping apart.

Three real differences from coins

  1. A different source of randomness: physical uncertainty on one side, a random number generator on the other. Neither lets you specify the result, but the sources really are different.
  2. A different level of attention: coins need a surface and six throws, which is slow; online gives you a result in a second, fast enough to invite careless casting. That, not the random number, is the real risk of casting online.
  3. A different way of keeping records: an online tool usually stores the result, while with coins you have to write the six numbers down yourself. If you want to look back later, online is the one with the advantage.

A sound online casting in five steps

  1. Write the question as one sentence — open-ended, about one thing, and within your own power to act on. See the five principles for phrasing a question.
  2. Pick a tool that shows all six lines. A tool that hands you a single verdict has taken away the thing you were meant to read.
  3. Cast the six lines once and do not recast. Decide before you click: this one is the one.
  4. Write the six numbers down, from the first line at the bottom to the sixth at the top. This is what lets you look things up and check them yourself later, without depending on that tool.
  5. Read in order: the judgment of the primary hexagram first, then the line text the number of changing lines points you to, and finally the resulting hexagram for direction. The rules are in the six steps for interpreting a reading and changing lines, primary and resulting hexagram.

How to tell whether a free online tool is good enough

What you pay for is usually the packaging of the interpretation, not a better hexagram. Five checks — the first three should be present, the last two absent:

  1. It shows whether each of the six lines is yin or yang, and which ones are changing.
  2. It gives both the primary and the resulting hexagram, not just one hexagram name.
  3. It includes the original judgment and line texts, so you can read and trace them yourself.
  4. It does not force a login or ask for an email before showing the result.
  5. It does not state the outcome as certain, and does not score you with a number or a percentage.

Four common traps when casting online

  • Recasting until you like the hexagram: the button is too easy to press. One question, one casting.
  • Asking the same thing many times across many tools: different results are inevitable, because each casting is an independent random process. It does not mean one of the tools is broken.
  • Judging by the hexagram name alone: the name is a label; the line texts are the content.
  • Treating "auspicious" and "ominous" as a verdict: in the classical text these words describe where a situation is heading, not a sentence passed on your life.

An honest note

The great advantage of casting online is the low barrier, and so is its great risk. What actually makes a casting useful is whether you are willing to write the question clearly, to hold yourself to one casting, and to read the line texts through. The I Ching does not predict outcomes and guarantees nothing. For medical, legal, financial or other major decisions, consult a qualified professional.

常見問題

Is an online I Ching reading as valid as casting with coins?
Coin casting works by taking six numbers out of a process you cannot steer: the coins rely on physical uncertainty, an online tool relies on a random number generator, and neither is chosen by you. So the material of the instrument is not the thing to worry about. What matters is whether the question is clearly written, whether you cast once and keep the result, and whether the tool shows you the full six lines, the changing lines and the resulting hexagram.
Can I recast online if I do not like the hexagram?
Better not. Online casting is one click away, so the temptation to recast is far stronger than with coins, and casting until a hexagram pleases you means you picked the answer yourself. The traditional practice is one question, one casting. If the question itself was wrong, rewrite it clearly, treat it as a new question, and keep the earlier result alongside for comparison.
Are free online I Ching tools less reliable than paid ones?
What you usually pay for is the packaging of the interpretation, not a better hexagram. Check five things: does it show the yin and yang of all six lines and which ones are changing, does it give both the primary and the resulting hexagram, does it include the original judgment and line texts, does it force a login or an email, and does it state the outcome as certain or score you. The first three present and the last two absent is enough.
Why do different tools give different hexagrams for the same question?
Because every casting is an independent random process and was never going to repeat. It is not a sign that a tool is broken. If you want to compare tools, compare whether they give you the six lines, the changing lines, both hexagrams and the original texts — not which hexagram came up.
