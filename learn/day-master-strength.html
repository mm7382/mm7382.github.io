身強身弱怎麼看？日主強弱與喜用神入門

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-19

學八字的人幾乎都會在某個時間點遇到這兩句話：「你是身強還是身弱」「你的喜用神是什麼」。這一頁把這兩件事從頭說一次：它們在問什麼、傳統上怎麼判斷、以及為什麼不同的老師常常給出不一樣的答案。

先講清楚兩件事。第一，這是進階主題，需要先知道日主是什麼與五行生剋才讀得下去。第二，底下寫的是本站對流傳解讀傳統的整理，不是唯一標準答案；這套語言在本站被當成反思與自我觀察的工具介紹，不主張它能預測事件，醫療、法律、財務決定請依專業意見處理。

身強身弱在問什麼？

日主是日柱的天干，代表命盤裡的「我」。其餘七個字對日主來說，不是在幫它就是在消耗它：

  • 幫日主的：和日主同一行的（比劫），以及生日主的那一行（印星）。
  • 消耗日主的：日主生出去的（食傷）、日主去剋的（財星）、來剋日主的（官殺）。

把兩邊放在天平上，支持多的叫身強（也寫作日主偏旺），消耗多的叫身弱（日主偏衰）。要注意的是，這不是在評價命好不好，只是替下一步「要扶還是要抑」找方向。十神的名稱如果還不熟，可以先看十神是什麼。

判斷強弱的四個依據

傳統上會從四個角度看日主拿到多少支持，常被簡稱為得令、得地、得生、得助。

  1. 得令：看出生的月支。月令代表當季的氣，如果月支和日主同一行，或正好生日主，就叫得令。這一項的份量最重。
  2. 得地：看地支裡有沒有日主的根。地支不只有表面那個字，裡面還藏著天干（藏干），日主的五行若藏在某個地支裡，就是有根。
  3. 得生：看天干上有沒有生日主的印星。
  4. 得助：看天干上有沒有和日主同一行的比劫。

四項裡拿到越多，日主越偏強。反過來，若月令是剋洩日主的五行、地支又沒有根，多半會判成身弱。

為什麼月令份量最重？

八字的四柱不是等重的。月支決定出生時的節氣環境，傳統上認為它對日主五行的旺衰影響最大——同樣是甲木，生在春天和生在秋天的處境完全不同。這也是為什麼排八字一定要用節氣換月，而不能直接拿農曆月份套，細節可以看農曆生日可以直接排八字嗎。

常見誤解：數個數

最常見的錯誤是把八個字裡的五行數一數，哪一行多就說哪一行旺。這樣算會漏掉三件事：月令的加權、地支藏干、以及天干地支之間的生剋合沖。一張盤裡木只出現兩次，但生在春天又有根，仍然可能是身強；木出現四次卻生在秋天、完全無根，也可能判成身弱。

喜用神是什麼？

判斷完強弱，下一步是挑出這張盤「最需要」的五行，這就是用神，連同輔助它的五行一起常被合稱喜用神。最通行的取法叫扶抑法，邏輯很直接：

  • 日主偏弱：需要幫手，取印星與比劫為喜用，忌過多的財、官殺、食傷。
  • 日主偏強：需要出口，取食傷、財星、官殺為喜用，忌再加印星與比劫。

換句話說，整套思路是把天平調回中間，而不是追求越強越好。

為什麼不同老師算出來不一樣？

因為取用神的方法不只扶抑一種，而且判斷強弱的界線本來就沒有統一標準。另外三種常見取法是：

  • 調候：盤中寒燥太偏時，先取能調節溫度濕度的五行。例如冬天生的盤先看有沒有火。
  • 通關：兩行相剋得太厲害時，取中間能轉化的那一行來化解。
  • 從格（專旺、從強、從弱）：某一行強到壓倒性、日主無法自立時，不再硬扶，改為順從當令的那一行。

同一張盤，先用哪一把尺就會得到不同結論，這是八字各流派差異最大的地方之一。遇到兩位老師說法不同，通常不是其中一位算錯，而是取用方法不同。

知道喜用神之後能做什麼？

主要用途是讀運。大運每十年換一柱，流年每年換一柱，傳統上會看運走到的五行是喜用還是忌神，來描述這段時間的順逆。至於把喜用神換算成幸運色、幸運數字、適合的行業或方位，那是後來附加上去的延伸說法，不是從上面這套推演直接得到的結論，看到時知道它的來源就好。

常見問題

身強比較好還是身弱比較好？
傳統讀法裡兩者都不是好壞的判斷。身強身弱只是描述日主在這張盤裡得到多少支持，用途是決定接下來要扶還是要抑，不是用來評價一個人的命好不好。
判斷身強身弱可以只數五行的個數嗎？
不行。八個字的位置權重不同，月令的影響遠大於其他位置，地支藏干也要算進去。只數字數常會把生在不當令月份的日主誤判成身強。
喜用神是什麼意思？
喜用神是依判斷結果挑出來、對這張盤最需要的五行。最常見的扶抑法是：日主偏弱就取印星與比劫來扶，日主偏強就取食傷、財、官殺來洩或制。
為什麼不同老師算出來的喜用神不一樣？
取用神的方法不只一種。扶抑、調候、通關、專旺從格各有適用情況，起手判斷的強弱界線也沒有統一標準，因此同一張盤可能得到不同結論。
知道喜用神之後要做什麼？
主要是用來讀大運與流年：運走到喜用神的五行時，傳統上視為比較順的階段。把喜用神當成幸運色或幸運數字的保證，是後來附加的說法，不是命理推演的結論。

Is my Day Master strong or weak? Day Master strength and the favourable element

By Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-19

Almost everyone learning Bazi runs into two questions: is my Day Master strong or weak, and what is my favourable element? This page explains what both are asking, how the tradition judges them, and why different teachers often give different answers.

Two notes first. This is an intermediate topic that assumes you know what the Day Master is and the Five Phases cycles. And what follows is this site's synthesis of the inherited interpretive tradition, not a single standard answer; the site introduces this language as a tool for reflection, with no claim that it predicts events.

What is the question asking?

The Day Master is the day pillar's stem, the "me" of the chart. Every other character either helps it or drains it:

  • Helping: characters of the same phase as the Day Master (Companion), and the phase that produces it (Resource).
  • Draining: what the Day Master produces (Output), what it controls (Wealth), and what controls it (Authority).

Put the two sides on a scale. More support is called a strong Day Master, more drain a weak one. This is not a verdict on the chart's quality; it only sets the direction for the next step. If the Ten Gods names are new, start with What are the Ten Gods.

The four tests

  1. The month: does the month branch match or produce the Day Master's phase? This test carries the most weight.
  2. A root in the branches: each branch also holds hidden stems, and the Day Master has a root if its phase sits inside one of them.
  3. Resource on the stems: is there a stem that produces the Day Master?
  4. Companions on the stems: is there a stem of the same phase?

The more of these a chart has, the stronger the Day Master. A month branch that drains or controls it, with no root in the branches, usually reads as weak.

Why the month branch counts most

The four pillars are not weighted equally. The month branch stands for the seasonal energy at birth, and the tradition treats it as the strongest influence on whether the Day Master's phase thrives. The same Jia Wood born in spring and born in autumn is in a completely different position. This is also why Bazi months change at solar terms rather than on lunar month boundaries; see Can you build a Bazi chart from a lunar birthday.

The common mistake: counting

The usual error is to count how often each phase appears among the eight characters and call the most frequent one dominant. That misses the month's weighting, the hidden stems, and the interactions between stems and branches. Wood appearing twice but born in spring and rooted can still be strong; Wood appearing four times, born in autumn and rootless, can read as weak.

What is the favourable element?

Once strength is judged, the next step is to pick the phase the chart most needs, the yong shen, usually mentioned together with the phases that assist it. The most widely used method is support and restraint:

  • A weak Day Master needs help, so Resource and Companion are favourable, and too much Wealth, Authority or Output is not.
  • A strong Day Master needs an outlet, so Output, Wealth and Authority are favourable, and more Resource or Companion is not.

The aim is to bring the scale back towards the middle, not to make the Day Master as strong as possible.

Why teachers disagree

Support and restraint is not the only method, and no standard line divides strong from weak. Three other common approaches:

  • Climatic balancing: when a chart is extremely cold or dry, take the phase that moderates temperature and moisture first. A winter chart is checked for Fire.
  • Bridging: when two phases clash hard, take the phase between them that converts one into the other.
  • Dominant and following patterns: when one phase overwhelms the chart and the Day Master cannot stand on its own, the reading follows that phase instead of propping the Day Master up.

Picking a different ruler first gives a different answer for the same chart. When two teachers disagree, it is usually a difference of method rather than an arithmetic error.

What it is used for

Mostly for reading luck. Luck pillars change every ten years and the annual pillar every year, and the tradition describes a period by whether its phase is favourable or not. Turning the favourable element into lucky colours, numbers, industries or directions is a later addition rather than a conclusion of this reasoning; it is worth knowing where those claims come from.

Common questions

Is a strong Day Master better than a weak one?
Neither is a verdict. Strength only describes how much support the day stem receives, and its purpose is to decide whether to support or restrain. It does not rate a life.
Can I judge strength by counting the Five Phases?
No. The eight characters are not weighted equally. The month branch carries far more weight than the rest, and the stems hidden inside the branches also count.
What is the favourable element?
It is the phase a chart most needs. Under support and restraint, a weak Day Master takes Resource and Companion, and a strong one takes Output, Wealth or Authority.
Why do teachers disagree about it?
There is more than one method. Support and restraint, climatic balancing, bridging and the dominant patterns each apply in different cases, and the strong-weak line is not standardised.
What do I do once I know it?
It is mainly used to read luck pillars and annual pillars. Treating it as a guarantee about lucky colours or numbers is a later addition, not a conclusion of the method.
