想學占星，怎麼挑學習 App？五個評估標準

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-10-15

占星 App 非常多，有的每天推送一段星座文字，有的提供完整星盤。如果你的目標是「看懂自己的星盤」，而不只是讀一段每日短文，可以用下面五個標準來比較。這些標準適用於任何一款 App，也包括我自己做的那一款。

學占星的 App 和星座運勢 App 有什麼不同？

星座短文 App 通常只看太陽星座，把同一段文字給同星座的每個人。學習型的 App 則會教你星盤的組成：行星、星座、宮位與相位，讓你能自己說出星盤上的每個位置代表什麼。兩種都有人需要，但想學習的人應該看它有沒有真正在「教」。

標準一：有沒有循序漸進的課程？

占星的學習有自然的順序：先知道星盤是什麼，再認識太陽、月亮與上升，接著學十二星座的四元素與三種模式，之後才是宮位與相位。檢查重點：

  • 內容是否有明確的先後順序，而不是零散的星座文章。
  • 能不能用自己的星盤對照所學。
  • 有沒有筆記或複習功能，方便回頭整理。

標準二：內容有沒有標明來源？

占星有很長的歷史，從托勒密的《Tetrabiblos》到十七世紀 William Lilly 的著作，再到現代心理占星，各家說法不同。值得信任的 App 會讓你分得出哪些是古典原文、哪些是後世解讀、哪些是作者自己的說法。來源清楚，你才知道自己讀到的是哪一種傳統。

標準三：星盤怎麼計算？不知道出生時間時怎麼辦？

星盤需要出生日期、時間與地點。好的 App 會說明它用哪一種宮位制，例如 Placidus 或整宮制，因為宮位制不同，行星落入的宮位可能不同。更重要的是出生時間未知時的處理方式：上升點和宮位需要準確的時間才算得出來。如果 App 自動代入中午或其他假設時間，卻不告訴你，你看到的上升與宮位可能根本不是你的。比較誠實的做法是清楚標出哪些部分無法確認。

標準四：是否把結果講成必然，或替你評分？

占星可以當成一套描述性格與處境的語言。如果一款 App 把未來寫成必然發生的事，或用數字、排名評比你和別人，你學到的只是標籤。比較適合學習的設計，是說明星盤上每個位置的意義，並寫出某段解讀是由哪個星象觸發，讓你自己觀察、自己判斷。

標準五：隱私和資料怎麼處理？

出生日期、時間與地點都是私人資料。下載前可以看看：

  • 是否一定要註冊帳號。
  • 星盤資料存在手機上，還是會上傳到伺服器。
  • App 是否需要連網，以及 App Store 的隱私標示怎麼寫。

怎麼快速判斷一款占星 App 適不適合自己？

先用免費內容試幾天，然後問自己：我能不能說出自己的太陽、月亮與上升各在哪個星座？我知不知道自己的太陽星座屬於哪個元素、哪種模式？我讀到的解讀，知不知道它從何而來？能讓你回答「是」的，就是適合你的學習工具。

常見問題

免費的占星 App 夠用嗎？
入門階段常常夠用。先看免費內容能不能讓你理解星盤的基本組成，例如太陽、月亮、上升與四元素；可以的話，再決定要不要深入。
不知道出生時間，還能用占星 App 學習嗎？
可以學行星所在的星座等不需要時間的部分。上升與宮位需要準確的出生時間，好的 App 會清楚標出無法確認的部分。
挑占星 App 時，最容易忽略的是什麼？
出生時間未知時的處理方式，以及資料是否上傳。建議確認 App 會不會自動代入假設時間、是否需要帳號。

想用 App 練習？

老實說明我自己的 App 怎麼對應這些標準：時來－星座用白話課程教你讀自己的星盤，前三堂免費，內容是星盤是什麼、太陽月亮上升，以及十二星座的三種動法與四元素。每一段都標明是引用原文、後世解讀、本課觀察提示、本 App 的做法，還是本課自行換算；引用的古典來源為公有領域的托勒密《Tetrabiblos》與 William Lilly 的著作。個人星盤採 Placidus 宮位制；不知道出生時間時不會代入假時間，上升與宮位保持空白。不打分、不排名；不需要帳號，資料只存在 iPhone，App 不連網。App 目前開發中，尚未上架，適不適合你，建議用上面的標準自己檢查。

How to choose an app for learning astrology: five things to check

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-10-15

There are a great many astrology apps. Some push a daily horoscope paragraph; others provide a full birth chart. If your goal is to understand your own chart rather than read a daily blurb, these five criteria help you compare them. They apply to any app, including the one I made.

How is an astrology learning app different from a horoscope app?

Horoscope apps usually look only at the Sun sign and give the same text to everyone born under it. A learning app teaches what a chart is made of, planets, signs, houses and aspects, so you can explain each position yourself. Both have their users, but learners should check whether the app actually teaches.

Criterion 1: Is there a structured course?

Astrology has a natural order: what a birth chart is; the Sun, Moon and Ascendant; the elements and modalities of the twelve signs; and then houses and aspects. Check:

  • Does the content follow a clear sequence, rather than loose sign articles?
  • Can you compare what you learn with your own chart?
  • Are there notes or review features for going back over material?

Criterion 2: Does it show its sources?

Astrology has a long history, from Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos to William Lilly's seventeenth-century work and modern psychological astrology, and the traditions differ. A trustworthy app lets you tell classical text from later interpretation and from the author's own view. With clear sources you know which tradition you are reading.

Criterion 3: How is the chart calculated, and what happens without a birth time?

A chart needs a birth date, time and place. A good app states its house system, such as Placidus or Whole Sign, because a planet can fall in a different house under a different system. More important is what happens when the birth time is unknown: the Ascendant and houses require an accurate time. If an app silently substitutes noon or another assumed time, the Ascendant and houses you see may not be yours at all. The honest approach is to mark clearly what cannot be confirmed.

Criterion 4: Does it present outcomes as inevitable or rate you?

Astrology can be treated as a language for describing personality and circumstances. If an app writes the future as something that must happen, or rates and ranks you against others with numbers, what you learn is a label. A design better suited to learning explains what each position means and shows which sky event triggered a given reading, leaving the observation and judgement to you.

Criterion 5: How does it handle privacy?

Your birth date, time and place are personal. Before downloading, check:

  • Whether an account is required.
  • Whether chart data stays on your phone or is uploaded to a server.
  • Whether the app needs a network connection, and what the App Store privacy label says.

How can you tell quickly whether an astrology app suits you?

Try the free content for a few days, then ask yourself: Can I name the signs of my Sun, Moon and Ascendant? Do I know which element and modality my Sun sign belongs to? Do I know where the readings I see come from? The app that lets you say yes is the right learning tool for you.

FAQ

Is a free astrology app enough?
Often, for the beginner stage. See whether the free content helps you understand the basics of a chart, such as the Sun, Moon, Ascendant and the elements, before deciding to go further.
Can I learn with an astrology app if I don't know my birth time?
Yes, for the parts that don't need a time, such as the signs of the planets. The Ascendant and houses need an accurate time, and a good app marks clearly what cannot be confirmed.
What do people most often overlook when choosing an astrology app?
How an unknown birth time is handled and whether data is uploaded. Check whether the app silently assumes a time and whether an account is required.
