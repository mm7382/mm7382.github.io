起完卦之後怎麼解讀？讀卦的六個步驟與取文規則

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-11

擲完六爻之後，解讀其實是一條固定的流程：寫下六個數字 → 畫出本卦、查出卦名 → 找出動爻 → 依動爻數目決定主讀哪一段 → 用之卦看方向 → 把讀到的寫成一句話。最容易卡住的是第四步，因為一卦有一段卦辭、六段爻辭，到底要讀哪一段需要一個規則。這篇把六個步驟走完，附上流傳最廣的那套取文規則對照表，以及一個從六個數字一路讀到結論的完整範例。

第一步：把六個數字原樣寫下來

三枚銅錢一次擲出，正面記 3、反面記 2，三枚相加就是 6、7、8、9 其中一個。擲六次，由下往上依序記下來，寫成像「7 8 7 8 9 8」這樣一排。這一步看起來多餘，但它是整個解讀的原始資料：只要數字記錯或順序顛倒，後面每一步都會讀到另一段文字。先寫下來，再開始想意思。

第二步：畫出本卦，查出卦名

7 和 9 是陽爻，畫成一整條實線；6 和 8 是陰爻，畫成中間斷開的兩段。由下往上畫滿六爻，下面三爻是下卦，上面三爻是上卦。兩個三爻卦疊起來，就能查出六十四卦裡的卦名與卦序。這時候得到的卦叫「本卦」，代表起卦當下的樣子。

第三步：找出動爻

數字是 9（老陽）或 6（老陰）的那幾爻會轉向，稱為動爻；7（少陽）和 8（少陰）不動。把動爻的陰陽翻過來，陽變陰、陰變陽，得到的新卦叫「之卦」。沒有動爻時就只有本卦，沒有之卦。動爻可能一個也沒有，也可能六爻全動。

第四步：依動爻數目決定主讀哪一段

這是最需要規則的一步。一卦有一段卦辭、六段爻辭，若沒有規則，很容易挑一段剛好合自己心意的來讀。宋代朱熹在《易學啟蒙》的「考變占」整理過一套做法，流傳最廣，也是本站起卦頁採用的：

  1. 零個動爻：讀本卦的卦辭。
  2. 一個動爻：讀本卦那一爻的爻辭。
  3. 兩個動爻：讀本卦那兩爻的爻辭，以上面那一爻為主。
  4. 三個動爻：以本卦的卦辭為主，並對照之卦的卦辭。
  5. 四個動爻：讀之卦兩條沒變的爻的爻辭，以下面那一爻為主。
  6. 五個動爻：讀之卦唯一那條沒變的爻的爻辭。
  7. 六爻全動：讀之卦的卦辭；若本卦是乾或坤，改讀「用九」與「用六」。

看得出一個傾向：動得少的時候重心在本卦，動得多的時候重心移到之卦。這套規則是固定的，不需要臨場判斷，所以每次結果都能被檢查。

第五步：用之卦看方向，不是看結局

「之」在古文裡是「往、到」的意思，所以之卦可以理解為「本卦往哪裡去」。它是方向的參照，不是結果的預報。常見的讀法是：本卦講現在的處境，動爻講正在改變的位置，之卦講這個改變朝哪一邊走。三者一起看，比只盯著一句吉凶有用得多。

第六步：把讀到的寫成一句話

最後一步常被跳過，但它決定這次起卦有沒有用。讀完主讀的那段文字，用自己的話寫一句：「這段話讓我想到目前的哪件事？哪裡正在改變？下一步我可以做什麼？」寫下來還有一個好處——過一段時間回頭看，你才分得出當時是真的呼應了處境，還是事後硬湊。

完整範例：擲出「7 8 7 8 9 8」

照上面六步走一遍：

  1. 數字：由下往上是 7、8、7、8、9、8。
  2. 本卦：陽、陰、陽、陰、陽、陰。下卦是離 ☲，上卦是坎 ☵，上坎下離就是第 63 卦既濟。
  3. 動爻：只有第五爻是 9，所以動爻是九五，一個。
  4. 主讀哪一段：一個動爻 → 讀本卦九五的爻辭：「東鄰殺牛，不如西鄰之禴祭，實受其福。」（《周易》，公有領域）
  5. 之卦：把第五爻由陽翻成陰，上卦從坎變成坤 ☷，於是之卦是第 36 卦明夷，卦辭「利艱貞」。
  6. 寫成一句話：主讀的那段在講「盛大不如合時」——東鄰殺牛的大祭比不上西鄰一份簡薄的祭祀，差別在時機對不對。對照之卦明夷「利艱貞」（在艱難中守正有利），可以寫成：這件事現在不缺誠意，缺的是時機與分寸；接下來可能轉入一段不順的時期，適合把事情做小、做穩。

注意這段結論裡沒有「會成功」或「會失敗」。經文給的是處境與分寸，結論是你自己寫的。

規則不只一套

朱熹那套流傳很廣，但它是一種整理，不是唯一公認的讀法。歷代有不同主張，例如一律先看卦辭，或以某一特定爻為主，各有依據。選定一套並標明出處，是為了讓結果可以被檢查，不是主張別人錯。重點是：知道自己用的是哪一套，而且前後一致。

常見問題

有好幾個動爻的時候，到底要讀哪一段？
照上面第四步的對照表：零動爻讀本卦卦辭，一動爻讀那一爻，二動爻以上面那爻為主，三動爻以本卦卦辭為主並對照之卦，四、五動爻改讀之卦沒變的爻，六爻全動讀之卦卦辭（乾坤改讀用九、用六）。
動爻越多代表變化越大嗎？
不是。動爻的數目只決定主要要讀哪一段文字，不代表事情變動的幅度。它是取文規則，不是強度指標。
解讀規則只有一套嗎？
不只一套。歷代有不同的取文主張。朱熹那套流傳最廣也最容易檢查，重點是知道自己用哪一套並前後一致。
同一個問題可以重擲嗎？
傳統上不鼓勵。重擲通常是因為不喜歡第一次的結果，那是在找想要的答案，不是在讀卦。比較有用的做法是回頭把問題改清楚，再擲一次。

一句老實話

這套流程的價值在於它是固定的：規則先定好，就不會每次挑一段剛好順自己心意的文字來讀。易經不會預知結果，也不保證任何事情發生。醫療、法律、投資這類重大決定，請找合格的專業人士。

How do you interpret an I Ching reading?

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-11-11

Once the six lines are cast, interpreting them is a fixed sequence: write down the six numbers, draw the primary hexagram and look up its name, find the changing lines, use the number of changing lines to decide which passage to read, use the resulting hexagram for direction, then write your reading as one sentence. Step four is where most people get stuck, because a hexagram has one hexagram text and six line texts, and choosing between them needs a rule. This article walks through all six steps, gives the most widely cited rule table, and works one reading end to end.

Step 1: write the six numbers down exactly as cast

Throw three coins together, counting heads as 3 and tails as 2, and the three add up to 6, 7, 8 or 9. Throw six times and record the results from the bottom up, as a row like "7 8 7 8 9 8". This step looks redundant, but it is the raw data of the whole reading: one wrong number or a reversed order and every later step lands on a different passage. Write it down first, then start thinking about meaning.

Step 2: draw the primary hexagram and look up its name

7 and 9 are yang lines, drawn as one solid line; 6 and 8 are yin lines, drawn as two segments with a gap. Draw all six from the bottom up: the lower three are the lower trigram, the upper three the upper trigram. Stacked, the two trigrams identify one of the sixty-four hexagrams by name and number. The hexagram you get here is the primary hexagram, the picture of the situation as cast.

Step 3: find the changing lines

Lines that came up 9 (old yang) or 6 (old yin) turn into their opposite; these are the changing lines. 7 (young yang) and 8 (young yin) stay as they are. Flip every changing line — yang to yin, yin to yang — and the new hexagram is the resulting hexagram. With no changing lines there is only the primary hexagram and no resulting one. A cast may have none, or all six.

Step 4: let the number of changing lines decide which passage you read

This is the step that needs a rule. A hexagram has one hexagram text and six line texts, and without a rule it is easy to pick whichever passage happens to suit you. Zhu Xi set out a widely cited scheme in the Yixue Qimeng (Introduction to the Study of the Changes), in the section on reckoning changes, and it is the scheme this site's casting page uses:

  1. No changing line: read the primary hexagram's own text.
  2. One: read the text of that line in the primary hexagram.
  3. Two: read both of those line texts, with the upper line as the main one.
  4. Three: take the primary hexagram's text as the main one and read the resulting hexagram's text alongside it.
  5. Four: read the two unchanged lines of the resulting hexagram, with the lower one as the main text.
  6. Five: read the single unchanged line of the resulting hexagram.
  7. All six: read the resulting hexagram's text; if the primary hexagram is Qian or Kun, read the Use Nine or Use Six passage instead.

There is a pattern: with few changing lines the weight sits on the primary hexagram, with many it shifts to the resulting one. The rule is fixed and needs no judgement call in the moment, which is what makes a reading checkable afterwards.

Step 5: use the resulting hexagram for direction, not for an outcome

The Chinese word for the resulting hexagram uses a character meaning "going towards", so it reads as "where the primary hexagram is heading". It is a pointer, not a forecast. The common reading is: the primary hexagram describes the present situation, the changing lines mark where it is moving, and the resulting hexagram shows which way that movement goes. Read together, they give far more than one verdict of good or bad fortune.

Step 6: write your reading as one sentence

This last step gets skipped most often, and it is the one that decides whether the casting was any use. After reading the main passage, put it in your own words: what does this bring to mind in my situation, what is changing, and what can I do next? Writing it down has another benefit — looking back weeks later, you can tell whether the text genuinely echoed the situation or you forced a fit after the fact.

A worked example: "7 8 7 8 9 8"

Running the six steps:

  1. Numbers: from the bottom up, 7, 8, 7, 8, 9, 8.
  2. Primary hexagram: yang, yin, yang, yin, yang, yin. The lower trigram is Li ☲, the upper is Kan ☵, and Kan over Li is hexagram 63, Ji Ji, After Completion.
  3. Changing lines: only the fifth line came up 9, so there is one changing line, Nine in the fifth place.
  4. Which passage: one changing line, so read that line in the primary hexagram: "The eastern neighbour's slaughtered ox does not equal the western neighbour's simple spring offering, which truly receives blessing." (Zhou Yi, public domain.)
  5. Resulting hexagram: flip the fifth line from yang to yin and the upper trigram becomes Kun ☷, giving hexagram 36, Ming Yi, Darkening of the Light, whose text reads "it is favourable to stay upright through hardship".
  6. One sentence: the main passage is about fitness of timing over scale — a great sacrifice of an ox counts for less than a plain seasonal offering made at the right moment. Read alongside Darkening of the Light, you might write: this does not lack sincerity, it lacks timing and proportion; a harder stretch may be coming, so keep things small and steady.

Note that the conclusion says nothing about succeeding or failing. The text gives a situation and a sense of proportion; the conclusion is yours to write.

There is more than one rule set

Zhu Xi's scheme is the most widely circulated, but it is one arrangement, not the only accepted reading. Other traditions argue for always starting from the hexagram text, or for giving one particular line priority, each with its own grounds. Choosing one scheme and naming its source makes a result checkable; it does not claim the others are wrong. What matters is knowing which one you use and staying consistent.

FAQ

How do I know which text to read when there are several changing lines?
Use the table in step four: none, read the primary hexagram's text; one, that line; two, both with the upper as the main one; three, the primary hexagram's text with the resulting one alongside; four and five, the unchanged lines of the resulting hexagram; all six, the resulting hexagram's text, with Use Nine and Use Six for Qian and Kun.
Do more changing lines mean a bigger change?
No. The number of changing lines only decides which passage you read as the main text. It is a text-selection rule, not a measure of magnitude.
Is there only one set of interpretation rules?
No. Different traditions select the text differently. Zhu Xi's set is the most widely circulated and the easiest to check; what matters is knowing which set you use and staying consistent.
Can I cast again on the same question?
Traditionally this is discouraged. Casting again usually means you disliked the first result, which is shopping for an answer rather than reading one. It is more useful to go back and sharpen the question, then cast once.

An honest note

The value of this sequence is that it is fixed: with the rule set in advance, you cannot quietly pick whichever passage suits you. The I Ching does not predict outcomes and guarantees nothing. For medical, legal, financial or other major decisions, consult a qualified professional.
