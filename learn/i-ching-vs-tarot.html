易經和塔羅有什麼不同？初學該選哪一個

作者：Wei Chun Chuang・2026-10-24

易經和塔羅常被放在一起討論，因為兩者都會用某種隨機程序產生一個結果，再由人去解讀。但它們的來源、手上拿的東西、意義怎麼來的，差別其實很大。這篇只做比較，不替你判斷哪一種有效。

各自從哪裡來？

《易經》（又稱《周易》）是中國古代文獻，卦畫與卦爻辭的核心部分一般認為成形於西周時期，之後兩千多年不斷有人加上註解與傳文，形成層層累積的文本。也就是說，今天讀到的「易經」其實是一部經文加上一整套歷代解釋。

塔羅的來源完全不同。塔羅牌最早是十五世紀的歐洲紙牌，一開始是用來玩牌的遊戲牌組；直到十八世紀末、十九世紀，才逐漸被賦予象徵意義並用於占卜，二十世紀初出現了影響後世最大的幾套牌面設計。相較之下，塔羅作為占卜系統的歷史短得多。

實際操作時你手上拿什麼？

  • 易經：傳統用蓍草，現在多用三枚硬幣。重複六次，每次產生一爻，由下往上疊成六爻，組成一個卦；六爻的組合共六十四種。
  • 塔羅：一副完整的牌通常是七十八張，分成二十二張大牌與五十六張小牌。洗牌後依某種「牌陣」擺出來，每個位置有固定的主題。

兩邊都有隨機步驟，但產出的東西不同：易經產出的是一個有編號的卦，塔羅產出的是幾張圖像加上它們所在的位置。

意義是怎麼得到的？

這是最實質的差別。易經的意義來自固定的文本：起出卦之後，你去讀那一卦的卦辭、六條爻辭，以及你選用的註解版本。文字是定的，變動的是你怎麼理解它。

塔羅的意義來自圖像加上位置：每張牌有一套常見的象徵意涵，但沒有一部公認的經文，不同牌組、不同作者的解釋差異可以很大；同一張牌在「過去」位和「阻礙」位也會被講成不一樣的事。

要讀的東西誰比較多？

易經的入門門檻偏「閱讀量」：卦爻辭是古漢語，通常需要靠白話翻譯與註解入手，六十四卦各有卦辭與六爻，內容相當可觀。塔羅的入門門檻偏「記憶與熟練」：七十八張牌的常見意涵要慢慢熟悉，牌陣規則則不難學，圖像本身也比古文直觀。

易經還可以不占卜就讀

值得單獨說明的一點是：《易經》在中文傳統裡本來就是經典之一，被當成哲學與文學作品研究，影響了後世的思想、文章與語彙，「物極必反」這類觀念就出自這個系統。很多人讀易經完全不起卦，只讀文本與註解。塔羅當然也有人研究它的藝術史與象徵學，但它沒有一部對應的經文可讀。

完全的初學者可以怎麼選？

  1. 想讀文本、不排斥古文、想順便接觸中文思想傳統 → 從易經開始。
  2. 偏好圖像、喜歡具體畫面、想要很快能擺出一個結構來想事情 → 從塔羅開始。
  3. 兩種都好奇 → 先各看一兩篇入門介紹，挑那個你讀完還想繼續讀的。

無論選哪一個，比較實際的看法是把它當成一種整理想法的框架：它給你一組固定的題目與角度，逼你把事情講清楚。不要期待它告訴你會發生什麼，醫療、法律、財務這類重大決定更應該去問專業人士。

常見問題

易經和塔羅哪一個比較準？
這不是本文能回答的問題，兩者都沒有被證實能預測結果。比較務實的問法是：哪一種材料你讀得下去、哪一種思考方式對你比較有用。
學易經一定要會占卜嗎？
不一定。《易經》本身是一部古代文獻，長期被當成哲學與文學閱讀，許多讀者只讀卦爻辭與歷代註解，完全不起卦。
兩個可以同時學嗎？
可以，但初學時同時學兩套詞彙容易混淆。常見的做法是先把一套的基本結構摸熟，再去看另一套。

What is the difference between the I Ching and tarot?

By Wei Chun Chuang · 2026-10-24

The I Ching and tarot often get discussed together, because both use some random procedure to produce a result that a person then interprets. But where they come from, what you physically handle, and how meaning is arrived at differ a great deal. This article compares them; it does not decide for you which one works.

Where does each come from?

The I Ching (Yijing, or Zhou Yi) is an ancient Chinese text. The core hexagram figures and line texts are generally dated to the early Zhou period, and for more than two thousand years afterwards writers added commentaries and appended treatises, producing a layered body of material. What people read today as "the I Ching" is a classic text plus a long tradition of interpretation around it.

Tarot's origins are quite different. Tarot decks began as fifteenth-century European playing cards, used for card games. Only from the late eighteenth and the nineteenth century were the cards given symbolic readings and used for divination, and the deck designs that most influenced later practice appeared in the early twentieth century. As a divinatory system, tarot has a much shorter history.

What do you actually handle?

  • I Ching: traditionally yarrow stalks, nowadays usually three coins. You repeat the procedure six times, each producing one line, and stack the six lines from the bottom up into a hexagram. There are sixty-four possible hexagrams.
  • Tarot: a full deck is normally seventy-eight cards, twenty-two majors and fifty-six minors. After shuffling you lay cards out in a spread, where each position has a fixed theme.

Both involve a random step, but the output differs: the I Ching gives you one numbered hexagram, while tarot gives you several images together with the positions they landed in.

How is meaning derived?

This is the most substantive difference. I Ching meaning comes from a fixed canonical text: once you have a hexagram, you read that hexagram's statement, its six line texts, and whichever commentary edition you use. The words are settled; what varies is how you understand them.

Tarot meaning comes from images plus position: each card carries a set of commonly used associations, but there is no agreed canonical text, and different decks and authors can differ widely. The same card is also read differently in a "past" position than in an "obstacle" position.

Which involves more reading?

The I Ching's entry cost is mostly reading. The line texts are in classical Chinese, so most beginners work through a modern translation with notes, and sixty-four hexagrams each with a statement and six lines is a lot of text. Tarot's entry cost is mostly familiarity: you gradually get to know the usual associations of seventy-eight cards, while spread rules are easy to learn and the images are more immediate than classical prose.

The I Ching is also read without divination

One point deserves its own section: in the Chinese tradition the I Ching is one of the classics, studied as philosophy and literature, and it shaped later thought, prose and vocabulary. Ideas such as a situation reversing once it reaches its extreme come from this system. Many people read the I Ching without ever casting a hexagram. Tarot too has serious students of its art history and symbolism, but it has no corresponding canonical text to read.

How might a complete beginner choose?

  1. If you like reading texts, do not mind classical language, and want to touch Chinese intellectual tradition along the way, start with the I Ching.
  2. If you prefer images, like concrete pictures, and want a structure you can lay out quickly to think with, start with tarot.
  3. If you are curious about both, read an introduction to each and pick the one you still want to keep reading afterwards.

Whichever you pick, the practical view is to treat it as a framework for organising your own thinking: it hands you a fixed set of prompts and angles that force you to state things clearly. Do not expect it to tell you what will happen, and for medical, legal or financial decisions consult a qualified professional.

FAQ

Which is more accurate, the I Ching or tarot?
This article cannot answer that, and neither system has been shown to predict outcomes. The more practical question is which body of material you will actually read and which way of thinking is useful to you.
Do you have to practise divination to study the I Ching?
No. The I Ching is an ancient text that has long been read as philosophy and literature. Many readers work through the line texts and historical commentaries without ever casting a hexagram.
Can you learn both at the same time?
You can, but two sets of vocabulary at once are easy to confuse. A common approach is to get comfortable with the basic structure of one before looking at the other.
