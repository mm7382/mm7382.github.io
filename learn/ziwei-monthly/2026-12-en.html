December Zi Wei forecast

Close the loop before beginning again

Read the monthly guide

December’s theme is turning a busy year into work you can hand over. December 1–8 falls in the tenth lunar month, and the eleventh begins on December 9. Use the first eight days to take stock, then deepen one commitment you want to carry into the new year. This is a planning suggestion, not a selection of auspicious dates.

WorkRelationshipsFriendshipsMoneyWellbeingAction plan

Work

Make your work easy to hand over

Using Tian Ji Quan as an image of coordination, separate what must ship from what can start next year. If three tasks are waiting on someone else, focus first on the one closest to a usable handover.

Try
Write a one-page handover with the result, open questions, owner and next date.
Avoid
Avoid promising new year-end work before confirming time and staffing.

Relationships

Say what you are hoping for

Using Tian Tong Lu as a prompt for care, choose one small thing both people can do. If single, notice whether someone follows through. If partnered, say how much time you hope to reserve for each other at year-end.

Try
Turn “you never spend time with me” into a concrete invitation, such as dinner on Friday.
Avoid
Avoid reading one late reply during a busy week as a verdict on the relationship.

Friendships

Keep friendship and commitments clear

Treat Lian Zhen Ji as a reminder about boundaries. Dinners, group orders and shared events can get awkward when nobody wants to discuss responsibilities until the last minute. Agreeing early can make the gathering easier.

Try
Agree on the budget, split and booking owner before sending invitations.
Avoid
Avoid accepting an invitation you cannot afford in time or money simply to stay in the loop.

Money

Start with fixed year-end expenses

Use Wen Chang Ke as a prompt for clarity. List next month’s bills, renewals and gifts before deciding how much December spending is flexible.

Try
Track fixed costs, flexible spending and reserves in three columns and review them weekly.
Avoid
Avoid treating an expected bonus as money already received or buying early because a timer is running.

Wellbeing

Leave space to rest between plans

Take the Tian Tong theme of care as a prompt to schedule recovery time.

Try
Leave an evening free between busy gatherings and keep your usual routine where possible.
Avoid
Avoid repeatedly giving up planned rest for last-minute events.

Action plan

Close one open loop

Bring Tian Ji’s planning and Wen Chang’s clarity to one list: one thing to finish, one to defer and one to carry into the new year.

Try
Set a date to finish and tell anyone affected what happens next.
Avoid
Avoid remaking every plan in December. Start with the one you can finish.

This issue draws on the traditional transformations for the Bing-Wu year: Tian Tong Lu, Tian Ji Quan, Wen Chang Ke and Lian Zhen Ji. We use them as prompts for care, planning, clear writing and boundaries. This is a public cultural reading. Use your own chart to see which palaces the transformations occupy.
