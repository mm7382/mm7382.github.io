Zi Wei Monthly Guide: every issue, and the two date boundaries people get wrong

Written by Wei Chun Chuang・2026-11-26

Short answer: the Zi Wei Monthly Guide is a free monthly reading. It takes the public imagery of the year's four transformations and turns it into six practical themes — work, relationships, friendships, money, wellbeing and an action plan. It does not predict events, it is not date selection, and it is not based on anyone's birth chart.

This page is the archive. It lists every issue, and it settles the two timing questions that make the monthly guide confusing at first: a lunar month does not begin on the 1st of a Gregorian month, and the annual transformations do not change on 1 January.

This page explains the structure and concepts of Zi Wei Dou Shu. It is not a personal prediction. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.

Each section gives exactly two conclusions: one thing to do and one thing to avoid. That is deliberate — you can act on it without first learning a vocabulary.

Boundary one: a lunar month does not start on the 1st

Issues are published by Gregorian month, so the title says "December". But the month boundary used in Zi Wei and the lunar calendar has nothing to do with the 1st.

A lunar month begins on the day of the new moon. A synodic month averages about 29.53 days — not a whole number, and not 30 or 31 — so the two calendars almost never line up. In December 2026, 1–8 December still falls in the tenth lunar month, and the eleventh begins on 9 December. That is why each issue states the correspondence at the top instead of treating the Gregorian month as a single unit.

In practice: treat the Gregorian month as the container for reading, and the lunar boundary as the turn in pacing. The first and second halves of the month are not asking the same thing of you.

Boundary two: the annual transformations do not change on 1 January

This is the one most often misread. The annual four transformations follow the heavenly stem of the year, and in this tradition the year begins at Li Chun, around 4 February — not on 1 January, and not at lunar new year either.

So one set of transformations carries across the end of the Gregorian year:

PeriodYear nameFour transformations
Up to Li Chun 2027 (December 2026 included)Bing-WuTian Tong Lu, Tian Ji Quan, Wen Chang Ke, Lian Zhen Ji
After Li Chun 2027Ding-WeiTai Yin Lu, Tian Tong Quan, Tian Ji Ke, Ju Men Ji

In other words, the December 2026 issue still uses the Bing-Wu set, not "2027's". For the shape of the year after the change, read the 2027 Zi Wei Annual Guide.

How to read an issue

  1. Start with the theme line. It is the summary of the whole issue, and tells you where the month's weight falls.
  2. Read one section properly. You do not need all six. Pick the one that is actually stuck this month and skim the rest.
  3. Turn the "do" line into an action with a date. Advice without a date does not happen.
  4. To apply it to yourself, use your chart. The guide describes public imagery; which palaces the transformations occupy for you is a separate question.

Three things not to misread

  • Transformation imagery is not an event. Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji describe tendency and emphasis. Any claim that a star makes a specific event inevitable goes beyond what they can say.
  • The guide was not written for you personally. It has no birth time and should not be treated as the basis for a personal decision.
  • Dates are pacing, not fortune. They order your reading and your actions; date selection is a different practice.
