Workshop: using the teaching example when your chart lacks a combination

When your own chart lacks a combination, practise on the teaching example instead of forcing it.

Original app lesson · free on the web

This lesson has been self-reviewed and is awaiting final sign-off. The classical passages have been checked word by word; the plain-language text and the exercises are written by this project.

A lesson describes a combination my chart doesn't have. Is something wrong with my chart?
01 · plain

In plain words

Each chart has only fourteen major stars and twelve palaces, so some combinations will be missing from yours. Some charts have no empty palace; some have no palace with two stars. Then practise on the example, rather than claiming your chart has what it does not.

02 · classic

What the text says

The text places the palaces by Volume 2 and describes the stars in Volume 1; the example is computed by the same rules from fictional birth data.

03 · inHouse

Compare with your own chart

Find whether your chart has an empty palace. If not, the app says so; then look at the example's Siblings and Children palaces instead.

04 · inHouse

An everyday example

If a lesson uses a pair absent from your chart, do not alter birth data or move stars. Switch to a clearly labelled fictional teaching chart and keep it separate from your own.

Use a labelled teaching example when the required configuration is absent.

TEACHING EXAMPLE

The example has two empty palaces: Siblings at You and Children at Wei. If yours has none, practise here.
