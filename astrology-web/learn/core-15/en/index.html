Trine and Square

By the end you will know how many degrees a trine and a square are, how to spot them on a chart, and how to read the two kinds of force: one comes easily, one takes practice.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

A trine joins one element group, like an easy gift; a square joins different kinds, like homework to practise.

01

A trine is 120 degrees apart and joins signs of the same group.

The trine

A trine joins signs in the same triangle, that is, the same element group: fire is Aries, Leo and Sagittarius; earth is Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn; air is Gemini, Libra and Aquarius; water is Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces. Each pair within a group is four signs, 120 degrees, apart.

Derived calculation

Astrology today often reads it as two forces that get on naturally, with no effort. With the Moon trine Venus, for example, how you look after yourself and what you enjoy fit well, so when you relax you are naturally doing something you like.

Later interpretation

A more useful question than “smooth”: are these two forces so easy together that you have never practised the other way?

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: Book I ch. 13 of Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos gives the trine one and a third right angles, four signs and 120 degrees, and calls trine and sextile harmonious because they join signs of the same kind — all masculine or all feminine. Ch. 18 adds that the zodiac is split into four equal-sided triangles, the first through Aries, Leo and Sagittarius — exactly four signs apart from each other.

Cited source
02

A square is 90 degrees apart and joins signs of different kinds.

The square

Astrology today often reads a square as needing effort to make two things work together. Take Aries (fire) square Cancer (water): one wants to charge ahead, the other to look after feelings first; both want to lead, so they take turns giving way or find a new way through.

Later interpretation

A practical view: a square is often where a person really builds a skill, because it pushes them to handle two different approaches. The longer it catches, the more solid the skill it tends to build.

Later interpretation

Ask yourself: in you, is it a place worth practising? Is there something that felt awkward at first and later became one of your strengths?

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: the same chapter gives the square one right angle, three signs and 90 degrees, and puts it with opposition among the ones that are not harmonious, again because the signs it joins are of different kinds. The text also explains the angles with musical ratios: apply a half to opposition and you get the square.

Cited source
03

Harmonious or not is a way of sorting, by what kind of signs are joined.

Harmony and tension are two categories

Any aspect can be read with two questions: do the two forces run smoothly, and have you ever practised the other way? For a trine the first answer is usually “smoothly,” so watch the second; for a square the first is usually “it catches,” but the answer to the second is often where your skill lies.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“Trines are good and squares are bad.”

A trine is a force that comes easily and a square one that takes practice; both are useful. Read an aspect by asking how smoothly the forces run and whether you have practised the other way.

“More squares means a harder life.”

A square describes two different approaches that have to be handled together, often where a person builds real skill. Each square can be read as a place worth practising.

“A trine is always within one element.”

Counting by signs, yes; but measured in degrees, a position near a border can fall in the next sign, where the element is different. This app measures degrees, so now and then you will see a trine across elements.

Limits of this lesson

An aspect describes how two forces get along; it is not good or bad and says nothing about luck. Astrology is a language for describing yourself, not a forecast.
