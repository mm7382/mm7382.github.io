Mark Your Tightest Aspects

By the end you can find the tightest aspect in your own chart and describe it using what each of the two bodies means.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Task: find the tightest aspect and write one line.

01

The aspect closest to its exact angle is the tightest.

Finding the tightest one

This app lists each aspect and how many degrees it is from the exact angle. The one with the smallest gap is the closest relation in the chart.

How this tool works

The natal orbs this app uses are 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, and 4 for sextile.

How this tool works

So a sextile half a degree off is far tighter than a conjunction seven degrees off.

Derived calculation

“Tight” simply means close to the exact angle. When you read the list, look at the gap figure and read upwards from the smallest; the two or three tightest aspects are the best places to write your first line.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 13 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy gives the four angles (180, 120, 90 and 60 degrees); ch. 24 says that when two places are not far apart, the one in front applies and the other separates.

Cited source
02

Say what each body is first, then how they get along.

Write from what the two bodies mean

When you write the line, first say what each body describes (from earlier lessons), then the relation between them. A line built this way has real content and says more than “I have a Mercury–Saturn square.”

Reflection prompt

For example: “Mercury is how I think and talk, Saturn is how I set limits and demands on myself; they are square, so I often check myself before I speak.” A common reading: a square is like two forces pulling against each other, a trine or sextile like two forces running the same way; a smooth relation is easy to use, while one that needs coordinating is often where you put in the most work and grow the most solid.

Later interpretation

To weigh the relation, ask two things: does it run smoothly, and does it take effort to coordinate? Those two questions let you describe the relation concretely. Either answer is a description, neither good nor bad; just hold it up against your recent experience.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: the book sorts aspects into harmonious and disharmonious, based on whether the signs joined are of the same kind (Book I chs. 13 and 12).

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“More aspects means a more complex, more capable person.”

How many aspects you get depends on the orbs used; the count is simply what the settings produce. Each aspect describes a relation between two bodies, and the relation itself is what is worth looking at.

“The tightest aspect is the main theme of my life.”

Tight means close to exact. The old text judges from many conditions taken together.

Limits of this lesson

The tightest aspect describes the relation closest to exact; it is a place to start observing and does not decide the theme of your life.

The orb figures are this app’s table: 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, 4 for sextile.

The demonstration figures are not a real person’s chart.
