Work With a Timeless Chart

By the end you can read a chart with no birth time as far as it goes, and write an honest list: what is settled, what has two candidates, and what simply is not there.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Your task: read a chart with no time as far as it goes, and write it as three columns.

01

Split the chart into three columns: settled, candidates, absent.

How far it can be read

Settled: the Sun and the slow planets usually sit here. Candidates: the Moon most often, and the fast planets on a day they change sign. Absent: the Ascendant, the Midheaven, the twelve houses, and every judgement that needs a house.

Derived calculation

With those three columns written, you hold an honest list of what you have. From then on, read any description using only the first two columns, so every line you read rests on real data.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: Book III ch. 2 of Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos takes the rising degree as the starting point, and admits the moment of birth is often unreliable — sundials shift and water clocks get stuck.

Cited source
02

The demonstration chart is clearly marked as a demo: a teaching picture for understanding the structure.

What the demonstration chart is for

With no birth time, the personal section still lists your own Sun and Moon, with both candidates when one changed sign on your birthday; only the Ascendant row uses a position marked as a demonstration, so you can see what an Ascendant looks like. The demonstration value stays in that row, and your data holds only your own placements.

How this tool works

So read that row as a teaching picture. Use it to understand where the Ascendant sits, then go back to your own first two columns to observe.

Reflection prompt

If one day you find your birth time, the house layer really appears, and you can read your own houses directly.

Teaching example

Demonstration, not a real person

Settled: Sun in Libra, Jupiter in Gemini, Saturn in Capricorn.

Candidates: Moon in Aries or Taurus; Mercury in Libra or Scorpio.

Absent: Ascendant, Midheaven, the twelve houses, and anything that needs a house.

These are fixed demonstration placements; the list shows the format for you to copy when you write your own. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“The demonstration chart is the app's guess at mine.”

The demonstration is a fixed set of teaching placements, and the screen clearly marks it as a demonstration; your chart comes only from your own birth data.

“Something in the third column means my chart is worse.”

The third column simply records which piece of data is missing. The first two columns can still be read and are well worth observing.

CHECK YOURSELF

Limits of this lesson

Read descriptions from the first two columns only; without a birth time the third column (Ascendant, Midheaven, houses) does not exist, so do not read it in.

The demonstration chart is a fixed set of teaching placements, not your chart; its values stay in the demonstration section, so write your own list from your own placements only.
