Find Your Three Key Placements

By the end you can find your Sun, Moon and Ascendant and write one line for each with the planet as its subject.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Task: find the three key placements and write one line for each.

01

Copy down your Sun, Moon and Ascendant exactly as shown, candidates included.

Finding them on screen

Start with the Sun, the Moon and the Ascendant: they are the placements most often used to describe a person, and the steadiest place to begin reading. Find all three and you have the material for three lines.

Reflection prompt

On the chart page the Sun and the Moon each have a row, with a sign and perhaps a house. The Ascendant has its own row, which shows its sign when you have a birth time.

How this tool works

Copy the three down before doing anything else, including any “unconfirmed” mark and both candidates. A candidate is what this chart truly looks like, and worth noting as it is.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 12 of the Tetrabiblos, listing parts of the body, Ptolemy gives the Sun sight, the brain and the heart; ch. 13 gives the Moon the sensory, non-rational part; and ch. 2 takes the rising degree as the chart’s starting point.

Cited source
02

The planet is the subject of the sentence; the sign describes it.

One line each, planet first

Write “my Moon is in Scorpio, so under pressure I go quiet first,” with the planet as the subject. If you have a house, add the area of life; if not, two parts make the whole sentence. “My Moon is in Scorpio” is more accurate than “I am a Scorpio”: a sign describes one body, not the whole person.

Reflection prompt

A common reading: the Moon shows what you need to feel safe, and Scorpio gives it a deep, held-in, watch-first manner, so under pressure it goes quiet first. Keep the sentence shape, change the body or the sign, and you have a different line.

Later interpretation

Once the three lines are written, keep them separate. Kept apart, the gaps between them stay visible — and the gaps are often the most useful part: a steady-looking Ascendant with a quick-reacting Moon, for instance, is a clue worth noting.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: the book judges a body first and then the sign it is in — for example Book IV ch. 4 reads Mars, Venus and Mercury and the signs they are passing through.

Cited source

Teaching example

A demonstration chart

Sun in Libra, seventh house: the energy goes into keeping relationships smooth.

Moon in Aries or Taurus (two candidates): how it reacts under pressure is not settled yet, so note both.

Ascendant in Capricorn: at first meeting, steady and a bit formal.

These are fixed demonstration placements. The Moon in the second line has two candidates: writing both into the line is the right way. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“The three lines should sound like the same person.”

The three describe different layers, so differences are normal — and that gap is one of the most useful clues in a chart.

“With two candidates, just pick one to make it easier.”

Keeping both is the accurate record; if you find your birth time later, it settles on its own.
