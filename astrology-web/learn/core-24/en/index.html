Reading a Whole Chart as a Person

By the end you can tie the first twenty-three lessons together and read a whole chart, in a fixed order, as a layered description.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Read a chart in order: first what data you have, then three key lines, then where they disagree.

01

The first step is to check what data you have and what is still empty.

First, what data you have

Ask yourself: do you have a birth time? If so, the house layer exists; if not, that layer is empty. Then see which bodies are marked “unconfirmed” with two candidates. Seeing this first lets everything you say later rest on data you really have.

Reflection prompt

This is the step people skip most, and the one that matters most. A lot of “it was inaccurate” comes from reading a layer you do not actually have; checking first avoids that trap.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 13 of the Tetrabiblos, on the quality of the soul, Ptolemy says its impulses vary so much that it cannot be judged simply or offhand, only by many complicated observations together. The old text itself asks for many observations and a careful judgement.

Cited source
02

One line each for Sun, Moon and Ascendant: which body, what manner, which area.

Then three lines

The steadiest order for a line is: which body, then in what manner, then in which area of life. With no birth time, skip that last part. One line each for Sun, Moon and Ascendant is enough.

Reflection prompt

Astrology today usually splits the work like this: the planet is the subject, the sign is the adjective, the house is the setting.

Later interpretation

Once you have three lines, leave them side by side. They describe three layers, and side by side is where they are clearest.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: judgement in the book always starts from a body: where it is and what it is connected to — for example Mercury and the Moon in Book III ch. 13, or Mars, Venus and Mercury in Book IV ch. 4.

Cited source
03

Where the three lines don’t match is what is most worth writing down.

Last, where they disagree

The usual reading: the Sun is the direction you want to be seen in, the Moon is the safety you need, and the Ascendant is the first impression you give.

Later interpretation

With three lines written, ask three questions. Does the way the Sun wants to be seen point the same way as the safety the Moon needs? Does the Ascendant’s impression match the Sun? In the tightest aspect in the chart, do the two forces work together or take turns? The mismatches are usually more worth writing down than the matches.

Reflection prompt

That is one full pass: data, three lines, gaps. The same order works on any question: work, relationships, a stretch of moods. Come back in a few months, reread the three lines, and see which one fits you best now and which you want to change. The sentence about who you are is yours to write, and you can change it any time.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: the book handles the relation between two places through aspects (Book I chs. 13 and 24); the relation itself is material for judgement.

Cited source

Teaching example

A demonstration chart

Check: birth time known, so houses exist; the Moon is unconfirmed, with two candidates.

Three lines: Sun in Libra, seventh house; Moon in Aries or Taurus; Ascendant Capricorn.

The gap: steady and formal on the outside, quick to react inside — that gap is what there is to write about.

These are fixed demonstration placements. When the Moon has two candidates, put both in the line, as the second line does. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“Reading a chart should give you a conclusion.”

Book III ch. 13 says this kind of judgement needs many complicated observations together. What a chart gives you is a few layered lines of description.

“Without a birth time this lesson is useless.”

The order still works; you just skip the house part. Checking your data and looking at the gaps need no birth time at all.

“If the lines don’t match, something is wrong with me.”

The three placements describe different layers, so differences are normal. A gap is exactly the material to observe: it often shows how you switch between settings.

Sources and rights

Limits of this lesson

Without a birth time the house layer does not exist, so skip it when reading.

Astrology is a language for describing yourself: reading a chart gives a few layered lines of description, not a verdict or a prediction.

The demonstration placements are not a real person’s chart.
