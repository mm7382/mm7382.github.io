Read Your First House

By the end you can read your own first house — the rising sign and anything in it — and know what to do with this lesson if you have no birth time.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Task: read your first house, or observe first impressions instead.

01

See which sign is rising, then whether any body sits in the first house.

Two things to look at

When you read the first house, look at two things: which sign the Ascendant is in, and whether any body sits in the first house. Write both as a manner — how you show up in front of others.

Reflection prompt

The Ascendant shows how you enter a room: your pace, your bearing, the first feeling people get from you. Aries rising, for example, often comes across as direct and quick to move; Cancer rising as warm, tending the mood first. If a body sits in the first house, a common reading is that its manner shows up in the first impression people get of you as well.

Later interpretation

An empty first house is common; it simply means no body happens to fall in that stretch, and has nothing to do with how much presence you have, so you focus on the rising sign.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: William Lilly’s Christian Astrology of 1647, in “Of the Twelve Houses,” gives the first house life, stature, colour, complexion, form and shape; Ptolemy’s Tetrabiblos, Book III ch. 2, takes the rising degree as the chart’s starting point.

02

With no birth time, observe first impressions instead.

With no birth time

The Ascendant needs an exact birth time. Without one, the Ascendant row on your chart stays empty; observe first impressions instead, which practises the heart of the first house just as well: which side of you people see first.

How this tool works

Do this instead: ask two or three people who don’t know you well to describe the first impression you give, write it down, and compare it with the lines for your Sun and Moon. If all three differ, that gap is what this lesson gives you.

Reflection prompt

If you find your exact birth time later, come back and compare. Having your observations on record first is what makes the comparison worth anything.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: Book III ch. 2 of the Tetrabiblos offers a method for working out the rising degree from the last new or full moon (syzygy) before birth, but it needs the whole set of domicile, exaltation and terms.

Cited source

Teaching example

Demonstration placements

Capricorn rising, nothing in the first house: enters steadily, a bit formal.

With Mars in the first house, that steadiness gets a forward push, and others feel the pace first.

Both describe a manner: how you show up in front of others.

These are fixed demonstration placements, made for practice; this layer exists only with a birth time. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

OBSERVE YOUR OWN CHART

Look at my chart

Your turn: read the chart if you have an Ascendant, or observe if you do not.

  1. With a birth time: note the rising sign, then whether any body sits in the first house.
  2. Without one: ask two or three people who don’t know you well for their first impression, and write it down.
  3. Either way, write one line: how far is that impression from the self you identify with?

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“The Ascendant tells you what someone looks like.”

The Ascendant describes how you enter: your pace, your bearing, the first feeling people get. To get a sense of someone’s Ascendant, watching how they walk into an unfamiliar room tells you more than their looks.

“No birth time? Just pick an Ascendant to practise with.”

Observing first impressions instead means every line you write has a basis, which is honest and useful.

Sources and rights
  1. William Lilly, Christian Astrology (1647)

    “Of the Twelve Houses” gives the first house the life, stature, colour, complexion, form and shape. Only the direction — this house is about you — is taken here, with the reading of appearance explicitly dropped.

    The 1647 text is in the public domain; the transcribing site's layout and notes are not cited. · 2026-10-10

  2. Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos, trans. F. E. Robbins (Loeb Classical Library, 1940)

    Book III ch. 2 takes the rising degree as the starting point and offers a correction from the preceding syzygy; that method needs the whole set of domicile, exaltation and terms, which this app does not implement, so no Ascendant is given without a birth time.

Limits of this lesson

Without a birth time the Ascendant row stays empty; observe first impressions instead, rather than using the Sun or any sign as a stand-in.

The demonstration placements are not a real person’s chart.
