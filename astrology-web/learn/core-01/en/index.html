What a Birth Chart Is

By the end you will know what a birth chart is, the three kinds of thing on it, and how to use it to describe yourself and check it against your own life.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

A birth chart is a table of where things were in the sky at one moment, seen from one place.

01

A chart records the sky at the moment you were born.

A chart is a table of positions

Think of the chart as a seating plan for that moment of sky: who sits in which section. When you read a chart, you find the seats one by one: first where the Sun sits, then the Moon, then where each of the other planets sits.

Reflection prompt

A chart records the direction of each body in the sky. This app works out which degree of the zodiac each body sits on — where it appears, seen from Earth. So when you read a chart, what you look at is which section each body sits in.

How this tool works

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 2 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy takes the degree rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth as the starting point of the whole chart; everything else is counted from there.

Cited source
02

Planets are who, signs are the style, houses are which part of life.

Three kinds of thing

The things on a chart are easier to remember as three kinds: a planet is the who, a sign is the style, a house is which part of life. Take “Sun in Libra”: the Sun is the who, and Libra is its style — talking things out and smoothing things over. Read any row by asking three questions: who? in what style? in which part of life?

Later interpretation

What sets them apart is whether they need a birth time. Planets and signs come from the date alone (on a day when one changes sign, the candidates are listed). Houses and the Ascendant depend on the Earth's rotation, so they need a birth time to be worked out. With a time, the chart gains the house layer; until a time is entered, that layer stays blank and the planets and signs can still be read.

How this tool works

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 4 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy sets out the nature of each of the seven planets one by one. William Lilly's Christian Astrology of 1647, in “Of the Twelve Houses,” names the first house the Ascendant, signifying life and the body.

Cited source
03

Astrology is a language for describing yourself.

Where the claims stop

Astrology is a long-lived symbolic language. It gives you words for describing yourself and angles for noticing things. You can hold those words up against your own life and see which fit you and which do not. Reading “Moon in Aries: under pressure, act first, think later,” think back to the last time someone rushed you: did you jump in, or stop and think?

Reflection prompt

As you read, the small tag beside each paragraph tells you what it is. Positions are really computed and can be used as they are; readings are handed down and are there to check against yourself. Look at the tag first, then decide how to use the paragraph.

How this tool works

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 2 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy argues that the motions of the heavens change the air and moisture around the Earth — seasons, tides, plants and animals shift with them — so inference is possible; ch. 3 then argues it is useful.

Cited source

Teaching example

A demonstration chart

Sun in Libra: this person puts their energy into talking things out and smoothing things over.

Moon in Aries: under pressure the reaction is fast — act first, think later.

Ascendant in Capricorn: at a first meeting they come across as steady and a bit formal.

These three lines use fixed demonstration placements, chosen to show how a chart can be read. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

  2. Check whether you have an Ascendant. A birth time gives you one; if no time is entered yet, a blank row is the normal answer.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“A birth chart is the same as a horoscope column.”

A horoscope column usually uses the Sun sign alone — one reading for a twelfth of everybody. A chart is the full table of positions at your moment of birth, and the Sun is just one row of it.

“The positions show how far the planets are from me.”

A position on the chart is a direction: which degree of the zodiac each body falls on, seen from Earth. That degree is what the app records.

“Without a birth time you cannot have a chart.”

You can. The date alone places each planet in a sign, and a body that changes sign that day is listed with its candidates. A birth time then adds the Ascendant and house layer.

Limits of this lesson

Without a birth time, the Ascendant and house rows stay blank; enter a birth time and that layer appears.

Astrology is a language for describing yourself, not a prediction or a test result; use its words to check against your own life.

The three example lines use demonstration placements. They are not a real person's chart and not computed from your data.
