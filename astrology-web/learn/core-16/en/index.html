Sextile and Orb

By the end you will recognise a sextile, work out how far off still counts, and use the orb to pick out the tightest aspects in your own list.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

A sextile is two planets 60 degrees apart; the orb decides how far off still counts, and this app uses 4 degrees for a birth-chart sextile.

01

A sextile is two planets 60 degrees apart, two signs away.

What a sextile is

How to spot a sextile: count two signs forward or back from the sign a planet is in, and that is where a sextile points. Two signs forward from Aries is Gemini, two back is Aquarius; 10 degrees Aries and 10 degrees Gemini are exactly 60 degrees apart.

Derived calculation

A sextile is often read as “an opening, but you have to act”: lighter than a trine, and it does not happen by itself. What the two planets stand for can help each other, but the ease shows up once you speak first or make the first move.

Later interpretation

When you find a sextile in your own chart, ask yourself: do the things these two planets stand for go especially smoothly when I take the first step? If you always wait for it to happen, you may never have noticed it.

Reflection prompt

In this app's aspect list the sextile has the smallest orb (4 degrees in a birth chart), so it is also the one most easily “just off, so it doesn't count.” The next section is about how far off still counts.

How this tool works

What the old texts say: Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos, Book I ch. 13, gives the sextile two-thirds of a right angle, two signs and 60 degrees, and counts it with the trine as harmonious because it joins signs of the same kind.

Cited source
02

Two planets need not be exactly 60 degrees apart; how far off still counts is called the orb.

How far off still counts?

The orb is the range for how far off still counts. Two planets 63 degrees apart are 3 off a sextile; as long as those 3 degrees are inside the orb, the sextile counts. The less it is off, the “tighter” the aspect; near the limit, it is “looser.”

Derived calculation

Books and websites use wider or tighter orbs, so the same chart can list a different number of aspects. When you see someone's list of aspects, you can ask first: what orb was this worked out with?

Reflection prompt

When you read your own aspect list, look at how far off each one is too: 0 to 1 degree is tight, close to the limit is loose. Start comparing yourself with the tightest few, then move on to the looser ones.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: Tetrabiblos Book I ch. 24 says that when two places are “not far apart,” the one in front is applying and the one behind is separating. So when two places are close, the relation already holds.

Cited source
03

Birth chart: 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, 4 for sextile.

The numbers this app uses

Birth chart: 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, 4 for sextile. Transits are read more tightly: 1 degree in general, 2 for the Sun and Moon, and “exact” means within 0.1 of a degree. These numbers are kept in one table with a version number, and every report records which version it used.

How this tool works

So the same chart may show a few more or fewer aspects somewhere else, and the difference usually comes from how wide the orbs are. Remember which table you are reading and comparisons stay clear.

Reflection prompt

Try it: pick one aspect in your list, work out how far it is from the exact angle, then compare that with the numbers above to see how far it is from the limit. The further from the limit, the tighter the aspect.

Demonstration, not a real person

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“An aspect only counts at the exact angle.”

When two places are “not far apart” the relation already holds; how far off still counts is the orb. In this app a birth-chart sextile counts within 4 degrees.

“A wider orb reads more accurately.”

Widening lets more pairs in, so the list gets longer. The less an aspect is off, the tighter it is, and you can start reading from the tight ones.

“Sextiles are weak, so ignore them.”

The old text lists the sextile side by side with the trine as harmonious. It is often missed because its orb is the smallest, so a little off and it drops out of the list.
