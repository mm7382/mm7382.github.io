Read Your Element Balance

By the end you can count the element and modality spread in your own chart, and explain how to use a spread as a hint for looking at yourself.

Approved original lessons, free on the web · 2026-10-12

THE IDEA

Task: count your own element and modality spread.

01

Match each body’s sign to the table and count how many land in each group.

How to count

Match every body’s sign to the table and count how many fall in each element and each way of moving. For a body marked “unconfirmed” with two candidates, leave it out of the total and note which ones they are; then try each candidate in turn and see whether your conclusion changes — if it does, the spread itself is not settled yet.

Reflection prompt

A spread is material to observe, and everyone’s mix is different. Its use is to show whether you keep reaching for the same approach.

Reflection prompt

Each grouping has names: the elements are fire, earth, air and water, and the ways of moving are starting, holding and shifting; crossed, they fill a table of twelve. A common reading: fire for action, earth for practical matters, air for thinking and exchange, water for feeling; starting begins things, holding keeps them going, shifting adjusts them.

Later interpretation

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 18 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy divides the zodiac into four equilateral triangles (the first through Aries, Leo and Sagittarius); ch. 11 sorts the signs into solstitial, equinoctial, solid and bicorporeal.

Cited source
02

The smallest group is the one most often misread.

Reading the balance just right

Two kinds of overreading are common. One takes the biggest group as an identity: “I’m a fire person.” The other takes a missing group as a flaw: “no water, so I have no empathy.”

Reflection prompt

Ten bodies split into four groups often leaves one group at zero or one; that is simply how the arithmetic works out.

Derived calculation

More useful questions: does your biggest group make you react too fast in some situations? Is your smallest group the part you have to remind yourself about? Both questions only ask you to observe yourself.

Reflection prompt

What the old texts say: the book treats these groupings as material for the rules that come later, used together with other conditions.

Cited source

Teaching example

Demonstration numbers

Of ten bodies: four air, three fire, two earth, one water.

By way of moving: five starting, three holding, two shifting.

You might write: handles things by talking and thinking, quick to start; the single water is a reminder worth noticing.

Fixed demonstration numbers: write the biggest group first, then the smallest, and one line of description takes shape. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

LOOK CLOSER

A common mix-up

“No water in my chart, so I’m cold.”

Ten bodies across four groups often leaves one at zero or one; that is arithmetic. A spread is a hint for looking at yourself.

“With two candidates, just count one; it’s tidier.”

Leave it out of the total, then try each candidate in turn, so you know whether that uncertainty would flip your conclusion.

CHECK YOURSELF

Now, try it yourself.

1. What does it mean when one element group is at zero?

2. How should you count a body with two candidate signs?

Limits of this lesson

A spread describes the approaches you use most, not a personality score; with a group at zero or one, such as a single water, you still have feelings.

Leave bodies with two candidates out of the total, then try each candidate in turn; each body counts once.

The demonstration numbers are not a real chart and not a model answer.
