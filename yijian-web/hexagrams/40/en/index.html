Xie · Release

The knot is loosening; act early and forgive what is past.

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Xie: The southwest is beneficial. If there is nowhere to go, returning brings good fortune. If there is somewhere to go, early action brings good fortune.

The Tuan commentary describes movement out of danger that escapes the danger, which is release. Going in the favourable direction gains the support of many; returning to the ordinary course finds the centre; acting early makes the going effective. When heaven and earth release, thunder and rain rise, and every fruit and plant splits its husk. The Xiang commentary asks the noble person to pardon faults and be lenient towards offences.

Kan below, Zhen above: thunder and rain together, the image of a deadlock loosening and tension letting go. The judgement says that with nowhere you must go, returning to the ordinary course goes well, and that if you do have somewhere to go, going early goes well. What matters is using the window while things are still loose. The lines begin with no fault at all, pass through catching three foxes, a lesson about inviting robbers, cutting an attachment loose, the release that makes the wrong people withdraw of themselves, and end with a hawk shot from a high wall. Releasing a difficulty means clearing the interference, letting go of what clings, and striking decisively at the one point that matters.

The judgment twice gives good fortune, in returning and acting early. The commentary's thunder, rain, and sprouting plants depict renewed life. The second nine's steadfast good fortune, fifth six's fortune, and top six's nothing unfavorable are positive. Yet the third six warns that possessing what does not fit one's position invites raiders, and early action is a condition, hence the rating: Good rather than the highest. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Worth doing: undo the constraints you have confirmed, treat old mistakes leniently, and finish the remaining items quickly. Avoid: relitigating a problem once it has eased, or dragging out the part that just needs closing.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Zhen above, Kan below: danger underneath, movement above — a structure of release.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Xie.
  • Contrast Xie with Zhun.
  • Work out that Release with its halves swapped is Difficult Beginning.

The lower trigram of Xie is Kan: broken, solid, broken. The upper is Zhen: a solid fourth line with two broken above. Only the second and fourth lines are solid. It uses the same two trigrams as Zhun, in the other order.

Zhun has movement below and danger above, so walking runs into a wall; Xie puts the danger below and the movement above, so the danger is already underfoot and the motion points outward — hence release. Note that xie does not mean everything is resolved, only that the stuck place has loosened by one notch and movement is possible. Where to go next is a separate question.

A teaching example, not a prediction: something stalled for half a year becomes workable because one approval is finally delegated. Xie describes that loosening; it does not say the rest goes smoothly.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Six at the beginning: No blame.

The line is two characters long and gives no verdict of fortune or regret. The Xiang commentary says that at the meeting of firm and yielding there is, by the reasoning of it, no fault, because the release has only just begun and nothing has been undertaken.

Two words, no fault, the shortest line in the hexagram. The loosening has only started and you have done nothing yet, so there is nothing to have done wrong. Staying quiet is the right response here.

In the first few days after a hard stretch ends, there is no need to plan the next move. Let yourself slow down. Doing nothing is perfectly fine.

Now that the pressure has lifted, are you letting yourself rest?

Nine in the second place: Catching three foxes in the hunt, obtaining a yellow arrow. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

The Xiang commentary attributes the good fortune of correctness here to having obtained the central way. In the hunt three foxes are taken and a yellow arrow is gained; yellow is the colour of the centre and the arrow stands for straightness.

Three foxes are taken in the hunt and a yellow arrow is gained, and holding correct goes well. The foxes stand for cunning interference and the yellow arrow for being centred and straight. Clear the noise, hold to honesty, and things run.

Deal with the few people or commitments that have been splitting your attention, then do the work the most direct way available. The difference in output is immediate.

Which three things could you actually clear off your plate today?

Six in the third place: Carrying a burden yet riding in a carriage invites raiders. Persistence brings regret.

The Xiang commentary calls carrying a load while riding in a carriage a shameful mismatch, and says that when you bring the soldiers on yourself there is nobody else to blame.

You are carrying goods on your back and riding in a carriage you have no business being in, so robbers come, and holding to it brings regret. The commentary puts it bluntly: you brought the trouble on yourself.

Putting on a display beyond your actual weight the moment things improve tends to attract trouble. Keep your position and your capability in proportion and you make fewer enemies.

Does the posture you are taking match what you can actually back up?

Nine in the fourth place: Release your big toe. Friends arrive, and trust follows.

The Xiang commentary says that releasing what is bound at the toe is needed because the position is not yet proper. What clings at the toe is a low attachment, and only after it is cut can friends be met in good faith.

You release the tie fastened to your toe, and only then can the friends who arrive be met with real sincerity. Some small attachments have to be cut before genuine allies can get in.

Let go of the old entanglements you keep saying yes to out of awkwardness. The space that opens is what keeps the people who actually suit you.

Which relationship are you still holding on to purely out of habit?

Six in the fifth place: The noble person achieves release. Good fortune; there is proof regarding petty people.

The Xiang commentary says that when the noble person releases, small-minded people withdraw. The release is tested and confirmed by whether those people stay or go.

The noble person who can undo what binds them goes well, and the sincerity of it shows in how others respond. The commentary is direct: once the release is real, the wrong people take themselves away.

When you set your principles firmly and cut what needed cutting, the relationships that depended on your wavering disappear by themselves. You do not have to drive anyone off.

Are the people still around you here for your principles or for your vagueness?

Six at the top: The duke shoots a falcon atop a high wall and captures it. Nothing unfavorable.

The Xiang commentary says the duke shooting the hawk serves to release disorder by removing what is causing harm, which is why nothing works against it. The archery imagery of the original should not be read as instruction for action today.

The duke shoots the hawk from atop a high wall and takes it, and nothing works against him. The equipment is ready, the position is high, the moment has come. This is a closing action, not the opening of a fight.

When the preparation is in place and the timing is right, settle the problem you have deferred longest in one go, instead of letting it become a permanent drain.

That thing you have put off the longest, are the conditions finally in place?

Quick Quiz

Which trigram is the upper half of Xie?
  • Zhen (one solid below, two broken above)
  • Kan · Water (broken top and bottom, solid middle)
  • Kun · Earth (three broken lines)
View answers and explanations for every option

Zhen · Thunder (one solid line below, two broken above) (Correct) : Correct. The fourth line is solid; the fifth and sixth broken.

Kan · Water (broken top and bottom, solid middle) : Kan is Xie's lower trigram.

Kun · Earth (three broken lines) : The fourth line is solid, so the upper trigram is not Kun.

What does xie refer to?
  • The stuck place has loosened by one notch and can move
  • All problems are solved
  • It means explaining
View answers and explanations for every option

The blockage has loosened enough to allow movement (Correct) : Correct. It does not mean everything is resolved.

All problems are solved : The hexagram says the structure loosens, without stating an outcome.

It means explaining : In the name, it means loosening or release.

How are Xie and Zhun related?
  • The same Kan and Zhen with their positions reversed
  • Xie is Zhun with one line flipped
  • They are unrelated
View answers and explanations for every option

The same Kan and Zhen trigrams, with upper and lower positions reversed (Correct) : Correct. Zhun has Zhen below and Kan above; Xie has Kan below and Zhen above.

Xie is Zhun with one line flipped : Four positions differ between the two hexagrams.

They are unrelated : They use the same two trigrams.
