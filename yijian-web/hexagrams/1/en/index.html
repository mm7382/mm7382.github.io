64 Hexagrams / 01

Qian · The Creative

Cultivate strength without ceasing, create through right conduct, and remain steadfast

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Qian: Supreme success. Steadfast correctness is beneficial.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Great indeed is the originating power of Qian! All things draw their beginnings from it, and it governs Heaven.” Commentary on the Image: “Heaven moves with vigor; the noble person therefore strives unceasingly to strengthen themselves.” (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Qian has six yang lines and symbolizes the initiating force of Heaven's movement. Its core is the four virtues of origination, success, rightness, and steadfastness. It emphasizes an active spirit of continual self-cultivation: advance through right conduct, seeking endurance rather than quick results. Abundant strength also calls for vigilance against decline after a peak, so that progress can last.

Qian is entirely yang and embodies all four virtues. First among the 64 hexagrams, it symbolizes the highest initiating power. With origination, success, rightness, and steadfastness all present, its overall direction is extremely positive, hence the rating: Highest Favorability.

Do: Set a clear direction, take responsibility, and keep improving. Avoid: Rushing ahead on momentum alone or turning conviction into pressure on others.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

All six lines are yang: both halves are Qian, with no broken lines anywhere.

  • Recognize that a hexagram with six yang lines is Qian.
  • Explain why exchanging Qian's upper and lower trigrams still produces Qian.
  • Work out that changing Qian's bottom line produces Gou · Coming to Meet.

Qian consists of two stacked Qian trigrams: the lower three lines are solid, as are the upper three. Counted from bottom to top, all six lines are yang without exception. Since both halves are identical, exchanging them makes no visible difference. For Qian, observe a single line changing instead.

Qian represents Heaven and the force that pushes forward and rises. Six yang lines describe strength at its fullest and least obstructed. This describes a structure, not a guarantee: all-yang lines mean that no part is holding back, not that the outcome must be good. Traditional discussions of Qian include many cautions precisely because pure forward drive can go too far.

Teaching example, not a prediction: A new project has just received resources and everyone's approval, with no conditions blocking it. Qian describes this structure of openness on every side. It does not tell you whether the project will succeed, only that nothing is currently applying the brakes for you.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Nine at the beginning: A hidden dragon. Do not act.

Commentary on the Image: “A hidden dragon; do not act, because yang is below.” Yang energy is newly emerging and concealed. The time has not arrived; build reserves rather than act rashly. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Strength is still accumulating and the time is not ripe. Do not rush into prominence or self-display. Prepare quietly and build your capabilities.

This is a time to accumulate strength and establish firm foundations, rather than rush to perform or make major decisions.

Are your capabilities fully prepared? What foundations still need strengthening?

Nine in the second place: A dragon appears in the fields. It is beneficial to meet a great person.

Commentary on the Image: “A dragon appears in the fields, its virtue spreading widely.” Yang virtue begins to show; a virtuous person should enter public service and seek out people of insight. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Your strength begins to show outwardly, creating opportunities to meet helpful people. Show your abilities proactively and seek guidance from those with insight.

This is a good time to exchange ideas with experienced mentors or teachers, demonstrate your abilities, and seek opportunities for cooperation or guidance.

Are your abilities ready to be shown? Who is your “great person”—a mentor or partner worth consulting?

Nine in the third place: The noble person works diligently all day and remains watchful at night. Danger, but no blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Working diligently all day means repeatedly following the Way.” The noble person knows when to advance and withdraw, staying cautious and disciplined; even in danger, there is no blame. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

During the busiest and most stressful periods, remain alert and disciplined. Avoiding both complacency and slackness helps prevent mistakes.

This stage requires close attention: work actively during the day and reflect at night. Do not become careless because conditions are favorable.

Do you still make time to reflect when busy? Where do you need particular care?

Nine in the fourth place: Perhaps leaping from the depths. No blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Perhaps leaping from the depths: advancing brings no blame.” At the threshold between advancing and withdrawing, you may decide for yourself. Leaping ahead and waiting quietly are both free of blame. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

At an important decision point, you can choose to leap ahead or wait quietly. Neither is wrong; the key is judging the timing and circumstances.

When facing an important choice, you may try boldly or observe for a while. The key is to follow your own evidence-based judgment.

What makes you hesitate about your current choice? What would moving forward cost, and what might it bring?

Nine in the fifth place: A flying dragon in Heaven. It is beneficial to meet a great person.

Commentary on the Image: “A flying dragon in Heaven: the great person brings things into being.” Yang virtue reaches its height. Someone with both position and virtue can accomplish great things and should meet equally insightful people. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

You occupy the most favorable position and have abundant energy. Cooperate and create with equally capable people. This is the hexagram's most ideal state.

With your condition at its best, actively seek important collaborations or new directions and move forward with capable partners.

Where do your current advantages lie? Who is a “great person” worth working with—a capable partner who shares your purpose?

Nine at the top: An overreaching dragon. There is regret.

Commentary on the Image: “An overreaching dragon has regret, because fullness cannot endure.” What peaks must decline, and excessive rigidity breaks. Only those who know when to advance and withdraw can endure. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

When developments reach their peak, remember that extremes can reverse. Excessive force or a lack of restraint brings regret.

Always advancing without knowing when to hold back, or insisting too strongly on your views, may come at a cost. Adjust your direction when needed.

Where have you become overly insistent or overextended? Is it time to adjust or yield?

Use of the Nines

Use of the Nines: A host of dragons appears without a head. Good fortune.

When all strengths emerge together, the absence of a single dominant leader can be auspicious. Collective wisdom is sometimes more powerful than individual force.

When many strong people share the stage, do not rush to stand out. Cooperation rather than competition often produces better results.

Quick Quiz

Which trigram is Qian's upper trigram?
  • Qian · Heaven (three solid lines)
  • Kun · Earth (three broken lines)
  • Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above)
View answers and explanations for every option

Qian · Heaven (three solid lines) (Correct) : Correct. The upper three lines are all solid, making Qian; the lower three also form Qian.

Kun · Earth (three broken lines) : Kun has three broken lines. This Qian hexagram has no broken lines.

Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above) : Dui's top line is broken. Qian's sixth line is solid.

What happens when you exchange Qian's upper and lower trigrams?
  • Still Qian
  • Kun · The Receptive
  • Gou · Coming to Meet
View answers and explanations for every option

Still Qian (Correct) : Correct. Both halves are Qian, so exchanging them changes nothing. Swapping trigrams is therefore not useful for observing Qian.

Kun · The Receptive : Exchanging positions does not change yang lines into yin. To obtain Kun, all six lines must change.

Gou · Coming to Meet : Gou is what results when Qian's bottom line becomes yin. That changes a line rather than exchanging trigrams.

Which hexagram results when Qian's bottom line changes to yin?
  • Gou · Coming to Meet
  • Lu · Treading
  • Tong Ren · Fellowship
View answers and explanations for every option

Gou · Coming to Meet (Correct) : Correct. The lower trigram changes from Qian to Xun, while the upper remains Qian. This is Hexagram 44, Gou · Coming to Meet.

Lu · Treading : Lu has Dui below and Qian above. Its broken line is at the top of the lower trigram, not at the bottom.

Tong Ren · Fellowship : Tong Ren has a broken second line. Lines are always counted upward from the bottom; the first line is the lowest.
