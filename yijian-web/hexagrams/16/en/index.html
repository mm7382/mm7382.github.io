Yu · Enthusiasm

Match enthusiasm with preparation, recognize change early, and avoid becoming absorbed in comfort

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Yu: It is beneficial to appoint lords and mobilize the army.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Enthusiasm: the firm receives response and its purpose is carried out; moving with receptiveness brings enthusiasm. Heaven and Earth move this way, how much more so appointing lords and mobilizing armies? Because Heaven and Earth move in accord, the sun and moon do not overstep and the seasons do not err. When the sage moves in accord, punishments are clear and the people submit. Great indeed is the meaning of Enthusiasm's time!” Commentary on the Image: “Thunder bursts forth from the Earth: Enthusiasm. The ancient kings made music to honor virtue, offering it abundantly to the Supreme Lord and associating their ancestors with the rite.” (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Yu has Zhen above and Kun below: thunder emerging above Earth symbolizes movement in accord with circumstances and collective inspiration. The judgment's appointment of lords and mobilization of armies emphasize organized, prepared collective action. The Image's music honoring virtue connects emotion with order. Yet the lines repeatedly warn against self-satisfaction, dependent expectations, and absorption in comfort. Joy needs restraint, and enthusiasm needs executable arrangements.

The judgment affirms conditions for organized action, but the first six warns of misfortune, the third of regret, and the top of dimmed awareness. Inspiration and excess coexist, hence the rating: Balanced. This is an editorial reading of tensions within the hexagram, not a definite forecast of good or bad conditions.

Do: Turn enthusiasm into preparation and rehearsal, arranging risk responses in advance. Avoid: Becoming absorbed in expectations while neglecting execution, or hiding problems behind a cheerful atmosphere.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Zhen above, Kun below: the only solid line is the fourth, just beyond the boundary between the trigrams.

  • Identify Yu's upper and lower trigrams.
  • Explain why a solid fourth line is read as the ability to get moving.
  • Work out that exchanging Yu's trigrams produces Fu · Return.

Yu has Kun below, with three broken lines, and Zhen above, with a solid bottom line and two broken lines above it. Its only solid line is the fourth, the start of the upper trigram. Zhen means movement, so the impulse to move occurs just beyond the boundary.

The lower Kun trigram provides a broad supporting surface; only the solid fourth line actually moves. Structurally, this means the base is broad enough to enable movement. Often described as joy or rising enthusiasm, a more grounded explanation is that this movement is possible through sufficient support rather than forceful pushing.

Teaching example, not a prediction: Registration finally opens for a long-prepared event. Preliminary details are all arranged, so it gets moving quickly. Yu describes this structure; it does not promise the event will succeed.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Six at the beginning: Proclaiming enthusiasm. Misfortune.

Commentary on the Image: “The first six proclaims enthusiasm; its purpose reaches exhaustion and misfortune.” Displaying joy can become self-satisfaction before one's footing is secure. The warning of misfortune reminds us that excitement can undermine proportion. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Displaying joy can become self-satisfaction before one's footing is secure. The warning of misfortune reminds us that excitement can undermine proportion.

When you receive some good news, confirm the conditions before making public commitments. Do not mistake a passing surge of enthusiasm for completed work.

Which achievements you are eager to announce still need verification or completion?

Six in the second place: Firm as a rock, not waiting out the day. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Not waiting out the day; steadfastness brings good fortune because of central correctness.” Remain as steady as stone, responding to change without delaying all day. This line affirms clear awareness, right conduct, and timely adjustment rather than wavering with excitement. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Remain as steady as stone, responding to change without delaying all day. This line affirms clear awareness, right conduct, and timely adjustment rather than wavering with excitement.

In an excited or persuasive environment, hold to your own standards first. If clear anomalies emerge, verify and adjust early rather than waiting for problems to grow.

What concrete signals tell you when to stop or change your approach?

Six in the third place: Looking upward for enthusiasm. Regret. Delay brings regret.

Commentary on the Image: “Looking upward for enthusiasm brings regret because the position is inappropriate.” Looking to others for anticipated joy can undermine independent judgment. Hesitating after noticing something wrong adds another layer of regret. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Looking to others for anticipated joy can undermine independent judgment. Hesitating after noticing something wrong adds another layer of regret.

If you keep waiting for motivation or approval from someone else, identify the next step within your own control instead of handing over every decision.

Are you placing your entire next step on one person's reaction?

Nine in the fourth place: The source of enthusiasm brings great gain. Do not doubt. Friends gather like hair held by a clasp.

Commentary on the Image: “The source of enthusiasm brings great gain; its purpose is widely carried out.” Being a source of inspiration gathers companions and support. The emphasis is mutual trust and clear direction, rather than one person keeping all the benefits. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Being a source of inspiration gathers companions and support. The emphasis is mutual trust and clear direction, rather than one person keeping all the benefits.

When initiating a project, explain goals, responsibilities, and ways to participate so partners can contribute individually, instead of relying solely on atmosphere or personal charisma.

Does your invitation include concrete arrangements that let willing supporters actually participate?

Six in the fifth place: Persistent illness, yet continually not dying.

Commentary on the Image: “The fifth six has persistent illness because it rests upon the firm. It continually does not die because its central position has not been lost.” A long-lasting state of constraint or discomfort still retains some support. Illness here is an image of circumstances, not a basis for predicting health outcomes. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

A long-lasting state of constraint or discomfort still retains some support. Illness here is an image of circumstances, not a basis for predicting health outcomes.

If an uncomfortable arrangement persists, identify what barely sustains it and address its constraints gradually. Being able to endure does not mean change is unnecessary.

Which constraint in the situation you are enduring most deserves attention first?

Six at the top: Darkened enthusiasm. Though established, it changes. No blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Darkened enthusiasm at the top—how can it endure?” Absorption in pleasure clouds awareness, yet willingness to change at the situation's end leaves the possibility of no blame. The point is an actual turn rather than further delay. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Absorption in pleasure clouds awareness, yet willingness to change at the situation's end leaves the possibility of no blame. The point is an actual turn rather than further delay.

When comfort or habit has trapped you, begin with a small, executable change and revisit responsibilities you have neglected.

Which habit of escaping reality are you willing to stop, and what concrete action will replace it?

Quick Quiz

Which is the only solid line in Yu?

  • The first line
  • The fifth line

The fourth line (Correct) : Correct. Zhen's solid line is at the bottom of the upper trigram, the fourth line of the hexagram.

The first line : A solid first line forms Fu, with Zhen as its lower trigram.

The fifth line : A solid fifth line forms Bi · Holding Together.

Why is the movement in Yu said to rest on support?
  • The lower trigram is all Kun: a broad base comes first
  • Because the upper trigram is Kan
  • Because all six lines are yang

The lower Kun trigram first provides a broad base (Correct) : Correct. The moving line is above, with the entire supporting Kun trigram below.

Because the upper trigram is Kan : Yu's upper trigram is Zhen, not Kan.

Because all six lines are yang : Yu has only one solid line.

Swap the halves of Yu. Which hexagram do you get?
  • Fù · Returning
  • Qiān · Modesty
  • Shī · The Army

Fu · Return (Correct) : Correct. Zhen below and Kun above form Hexagram 24, Fu. The solid line moves from the fourth to the first position.

Qiān · Modesty : Modesty has a solid third line, with Gen below rather than Kun.

Shī · The Army : Shi · The Army has Kan below and Kun above, with a solid second line.
