Zhun · Difficulty at the Beginning

Beginnings are hard; build roots before reach

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Zhun: Supreme success; steadfast correctness is beneficial. Do not set out to proceed. It is beneficial to appoint lords.

The Tuan commentary says that when firm and yielding first meet, difficulty is born; there is movement inside danger. The Xiang commentary reads cloud and thunder as the image, and asks the noble person to bring order out of tangle.

Zhun is the image of a shoot forcing its way up through the soil. Thunder's energy sits beneath the water of danger — there is plenty of force, but the way out is blocked. The judgement says the start is fundamentally sound, yet also warns against setting off anywhere. Build a base first and let the timing catch up.

Although Zhun offers supreme success, the judgment explicitly warns against setting out, indicating obstacles and risks in action. Many lines are difficult but not without an exit; hope remains amid hardship. Its overall direction is neutral with caution, hence the rating: Balanced.

Worth doing: start small, get your resources in place, and find people you can rely on. Avoid: expanding from day one, or changing direction every time the picture looks messy.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Kan above, Zhen below: movement underneath, danger on top — the structure of a hard beginning.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Zhun.
  • Explain how movement below and danger above reads as a difficult start.
  • Say that swapping the halves turns Difficult Beginning into Release.

The lower trigram of Zhun is Zhen: one yang at the bottom with two yin above it. The upper is Kan: one yang in the middle with yin on both sides. Read from the bottom up, the figure moves first and immediately runs into Kan. Both trigrams have just one solid line; their different positions make their meanings entirely different.

Zhen is movement, the first stirring; Kan is a pit, being caught. Zhun puts movement below and danger above, picturing a situation that has already set off before the road ahead has been cleared. It is often glossed as “beginnings are hard”, but closer to the structure is this: at this stage drive is not what is missing — direction and a foothold are.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a team has started building, but the specification is unsettled and responsibilities are undivided. Effort goes out and keeps hitting walls. Zhun describes that structure; it does not say the work is doomed.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Nine at the beginning: Lingering in hesitation. Remaining steadfast is beneficial; appointing lords is beneficial.

The Xiang commentary says that although there is hesitation, the intent remains correct. Placing the worthy below the common people wins them over.

You are circling rather than advancing, and that caution is right. Holding your position and building a base matters more than moving now.

If you feel stuck in place, do not panic. This is a good stretch for accumulating strength — make the groundwork solid first.

Do you need to charge ahead, or firm up what is under your feet, and what counts as your foundation?

Six in the second place: Difficulty and turning back, horses separating. Not robbers, but suitors. The woman remains steadfast and does not marry; after ten years, she marries.

The Xiang commentary attributes the difficulty here to the yielding line riding above a firm one. Ten years before the union says the delay is long but not permanent.

The road forward is full of obstacles and nothing goes as planned. What looks like a threat may be an opportunity, but clarity takes a long time to arrive.

Things feel blocked and unresolved. You need real patience — do not walk away simply because the short term shows nothing.

Have you given up too early on something that needed a long wait, and was it worth waiting for?

Six in the third place: Pursuing deer without a forester, only entering the woods. The noble person sees the signs: better to give up. Advancing brings regret.

The Xiang commentary says chasing deer without a forester is merely running after game. The noble person lets it go, since going on leads only to a dead end.

Chasing a target with no guide takes you deep into the woods with no way out. The wise choice is to stop; pressing on costs you.

If you are pursuing something and still cannot find a route, pausing to reassess beats pushing blindly onward.

Do you have a reliable method or guide for what you are chasing, or are you just going on instinct?

Six in the fourth place: Horses separating. Seeking marriage, go forward. Good fortune; nothing unfavorable.

The Xiang commentary says that to seek and then go forward shows clear sight. Reaching out for the right alliance is the sound move.

Go and seek the partnership or connection that fits. Moving forward here is favourable, with nothing working against you. This is the moment to take the initiative.

If there is a collaboration or relationship worth pursuing, act now. Do not let earlier hesitation cost you this one.

Which opportunity or relationship have you been hesitating over, and what is holding you back?

Nine in the fifth place: Hoarding the nourishing grease. Small steadfastness brings good fortune; great steadfastness brings misfortune.

The Xiang commentary says the stored richness cannot yet shine out. The honoured position is hemmed in, so small matters succeed while large ones do not.

You hold resources but cannot deploy them widely. Holding firm on a small scale works; overreaching does not. Match the task to what you actually have.

Your current energy and resources are enough for what is in front of you. Do not widen the scope or raise the target yet — finish what exists.

Has your target outgrown the capability and resources you actually have?

Six at the top: Horses separating; tears of blood flow endlessly.

The Xiang commentary asks how weeping tears of blood could possibly last. Hardship has piled up to its limit, and something has to change.

Long circling inside difficulty has worn your reserves to the edge. What matters now is asking for help or changing course, not gritting through.

If you have been under heavy pressure for a long time with no improvement, reach out for support or rethink the strategy. Enduring alone is not the answer.

What long-running pressure are you carrying, and are you willing to ask someone for help?

Quick Quiz

Which trigram is the upper half of Zhun?
  • Kan · Water (broken top and bottom, solid middle)
  • Zhen (one solid below, two broken above)
  • Kun · Earth (three broken lines)
View answers and explanations for every option

Kan · Water (broken top and bottom, solid middle) (Correct) : Correct. Broken fourth, solid fifth, broken sixth: Kan.

Zhen (one solid below, two broken above) : Zhen is Zhun's lower trigram. Counted upward, Zhen has a solid first line.

Kun · Earth (three broken lines) : Kun has three broken lines. Zhun's fifth line is solid.

What do “movement below, danger above” refer to in Zhun?
  • The lower Zhen is movement and the upper Kan is danger
  • The upper Zhen is movement and the lower Kan is danger
  • Both trigrams are danger
View answers and explanations for every option

Lower Zhen is movement; upper Kan is danger (Correct) : Correct. Reading upward, Zhen's movement comes first, then encounters Kan.

The upper Zhen is movement and the lower Kan is danger : The positions are reversed. Zhun has Zhen below and Kan above. Exchanging them gives Kan below and Zhen above: Xie.

Both trigrams are danger : Two Kan trigrams form Kan, Hexagram 29. Zhun has Kan only above.

Swap the halves of Zhun. Which hexagram do you get?
  • Xiè · Release
  • Méng · Youthful Ignorance
  • Xū · Waiting
View answers and explanations for every option

Xie · Release (Correct) : Correct. Kan below and Zhen above form Hexagram 40, Xie, the same two trigrams as Zhun in exchanged positions.

Méng · Youthful Ignorance : Meng has Kan below and Gen above; its upper trigram is not Zhen.

Xū · Waiting : Xu has Qian below and Kan above; its lower trigram is not Kan.
