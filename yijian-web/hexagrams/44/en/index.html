Gou · Coming to Meet

In a chance meeting, look closely before you commit.

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Gou: The woman is strong. Do not take this woman as a wife.

The Tuan commentary glosses Gou as meeting, the yielding encountering the firm. In the marriage image the union is not one to keep, yet from the wider view heaven and earth meeting is what makes all things manifest, and firmness meeting the centred and correct lets the way travel widely; hence the exclamation that the meaning of this moment is great. The Xiang image is wind beneath heaven reaching everywhere, with the ruler issuing commands to the four quarters. The marriage language belongs to the period and should not be read as a judgement on people today.

Qian above, Xun below. Wind travels under heaven and reaches everywhere, so this is the hexagram of the unplanned encounter. A single yielding line appears at the bottom and begins meeting the five firm lines above it, which is why the judgement uses a marriage figure to counsel against a hasty union. The lines move from a brake fastened on the wheel, through the contrast of a wrapping that holds a fish and one that does not, a hesitant advance that avoids real fault, a melon sheltered under leaves with something dropping from the sky, and a meeting at the horn that brings only a small regret.

The judgment explicitly discourages marriage, and the first six's misfortune in proceeding and fourth nine's arising misfortune are negative. Yet the second and top nines have no blame, the fifth's concealed excellence and gift falling from Heaven suggest unexpected gain, and the commentary strongly affirms the significance of this meeting's time. Warnings and opportunities have comparable weight, hence the rating: Neutral. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Worth doing: identify what a sudden opportunity actually is, and set limits before you decide how far to engage. Avoid: promising on the strength of novelty, or underestimating how quickly a small problem spreads.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Qian above, Xun below: one yin line has just entered at the bottom, with five solid lines above.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Gou.
  • Contrast Gou with Guai.
  • Point out that Encountering and Small Restraint use the same two trigrams in the other order.

The lower trigram of Gou is Xun: a broken first line with two solid above. The upper is Qian, all solid. Only the first line of the figure is broken. It is also the hexagram you get by flipping the bottom line of Qian.

In Guai one yin line remains at the top; in Gou one has just entered at the bottom. The difference in position matters: the newcomer is at the base, smallest in quantity yet sitting in the foundation. This hexagram concerns encountering — something new has appeared, not yet enough to change what is above, but it has entered at the deepest point. Noticing it matters more than its size.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a system shows one rare error with little impact, but it occurs in the lowest-level component. Gou describes that structure.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Six at the beginning: Tied to a metal brake. Steadfastness brings good fortune; proceeding brings misfortune. A lean pig strains restlessly.

The Xiang commentary says that fastening the metal brake shows the yielding way being held in check. Going forward meets misfortune, and a lean pig fretting and pacing supplies the image.

Stop as a metal brake stops a wheel, and holding correct reads well; hurry forward and you meet trouble. What the first stirring needs is restraint rather than movement.

With an opportunity or a connection that has only just appeared, hold still and look properly first. Restraint at this stage protects you more than enthusiasm does.

Do you want this new opening because you understand it, or because it is new?

Nine in the second place: Fish in the wrapping. No blame. Unfavorable for guests.

The Xiang commentary says that the fish in the wrapping is, in principle, not to be extended to guests: what belongs to a close relation is not for wider distribution.

There is a fish in the wrapping and no fault in it, but it is not to be shared out to guests. What you hold falls within your own proper sphere; keeping it well is enough, and broadcasting it is neither necessary nor wise.

When you have something real in hand, hold it steadily and look after the people directly involved. Spreading it widely at this point tends to create disputes.

How far out does it make sense to share what you are holding?

Nine in the third place: No skin on the buttocks; walking falters. Danger, but no great blame.

The Xiang commentary explains the hesitant gait as an advance that has not yet found any pull or support, so there is danger without great fault.

Uneasy sitting still and hesitant walking forward: the position is hard, but because you have not actually stepped out there is no great fault. Staying where you are is uncomfortable and it is also what protects you now.

When you feel stuck between two directions and no one is there to pull you through, pausing is not failure. Forcing the step forward is what turns a small problem into a real loss.

Is your hesitation a lack of nerve, or your instinct registering the risk first?

Nine in the fourth place: No fish in the wrapping. Misfortune arises.

The Xiang commentary attributes the misfortune of the empty wrapping to having grown distant from those below, so the answering base is gone.

The wrapping is opened and there is no fish, and trouble arises from that. Set against the earlier line, the difference is whether the connection was maintained. The commentary puts the cause in distance from the people below.

When support you counted on suddenly is not there, it is usually the result of neglect over time. The repair is to go back and rebuild the relationship, not to look elsewhere for a substitute.

Which once-close relationship have you left untended for too long?

Nine in the fifth place: Wrap the melon with willow. Concealed excellence; something falls from Heaven.

The Xiang commentary reads the hidden pattern as centred correctness, and the thing falling from the sky as a will that does not abandon what it has been given. The melon is wrapped in leaves of the qi tree.

Broad leaves shelter the melon beneath, the fine quality stays hidden rather than displayed, and something arrives from above. This is the strongest line in the hexagram, and it turns on quiet correctness rather than visible effort.

Keep your capability out of the shop window and protect the people and work that deserve protecting, without pushing for recognition. Trust built this way tends to bring openings when you are not looking.

What care do you put in that you would keep up even if nobody noticed?

Nine at the top: Meeting with the horns. Regret, but no blame.

The Xiang commentary says that meeting at the horn is the regret of being at the top and at the limit; since no one else is to blame, there is still no fault.

The meeting happens at the highest and hardest point, which brings regret but no fault. Standing high and rigid leaves fewer people to meet. That comes from the position, not from anyone's failing.

Past a certain level you find there are fewer people you can speak to openly. That loneliness belongs to the position, and accepting it is more useful than blaming others for keeping their distance.

Is your current isolation people leaving, or simply how few stand where you stand?

Quick Quiz

How do Gou and Guai contrast?
  • Guai has its yin line at the top, Gou at the bottom
  • They have different numbers of yin lines
  • Gou is Guai with its halves swapped
View answers and explanations for every option

Guai's yin line is at the top; Gou's is at the bottom (Correct) : Correct. Both have five solid lines and one broken line, at opposite ends.

They have different numbers of yin lines : Both have only one broken line.

Gou is Guai with its halves swapped : Exchanging Guai's trigrams produces Lu · Treading, not Gou.

Why is the yin line of Gou worth noticing?
  • It is smallest in quantity but sits in the foundation
  • Because it will grow at once
  • Because it is the sixth line
View answers and explanations for every option

The smallest amount, but positioned in the foundation (Correct) : Correct. It occupies the first line, at the structure's very bottom.

Because it will grow at once : The diagram describes the present arrangement, not the next step.

Because it is the sixth line : Gou's sixth line is solid.

Flipping the first line of Qian gives which hexagram?
  • Gou · Coming to Meet
  • Guai · Breakthrough
  • Xiao Xu · Small Accumulation

Gou · Coming to Meet (Correct) : Correct. The lower trigram changes from Qian to Xun.

Guai · Breakthrough : Guai's broken line is sixth.

Xiao Xu · Small Accumulation : Xiao Xu's broken line is fourth.
