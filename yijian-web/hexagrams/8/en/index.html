64 Hexagrams / 08

Bi · Holding Together

Reach out sincerely; lasting bonds carry you

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Bi: Good fortune. Consult the original divination for fundamental, lasting steadfastness; no blame. The unsettled come from all sides. The latecomer meets misfortune.

The Tuan commentary calls Bi favourable and defines it as support, with those below following willingly. Those who arrive late find their way exhausted. The Xiang commentary sees water on the earth, and the early kings establishing states and drawing the lords close.

Bi is water spread over the earth — things resting on one another and nourishing each other in the process. It is the hexagram of alliance and mutual support, of the ties between people and between groups. Everything here turns on sincerity: only honest intent builds a real bond. Move towards the upright people first and commit for the long term. Those who hesitate and arrive late may find the moment gone.

Bi's judgment begins with good fortune, and fundamental, lasting steadfastness without blame emphasizes the basis of enduring fortune. Its spirit is sincere connection and mutual help, creating a positive overall atmosphere, hence the rating: Highly Favorable.

Worth doing: offer goodwill first, and commit long term to people whose values are close to yours. Avoid: attaching yourself to a group just to belong, or joining a circle that defines itself by exclusion.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Kan above, Kun below: the many underneath, danger on top, with the one solid line raised to the fifth place.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Bi.
  • State how Bi differs from Shi.
  • Say why the fifth position is worth noticing.

The lower trigram of Bi is Kun and the upper is Kan — the reverse of Shi. The figure again has a single solid line, but it falls on the fifth line, the middle of the upper trigram. Five broken lines surround it, and the centre of gravity of the whole structure sits there.

The fifth line is often treated as the place that is both central and high, so although Bi and Shi use the same two trigrams they read quite differently: in Shi the leverage is low down and has to organise; in Bi it is above, and the many gather toward it. Note that this is structural affiliation — it does not promise that gathering will turn out well.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a new unit forms around a lead with real practical credibility, and people are willing to follow that judgement for now. Bi describes that structure of gathering toward a centre; it does not mean the judgement is right.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Six at the beginning: With sincerity, join them. No blame. Sincerity fills the earthen jar; in the end, something more comes. Good fortune.

The Xiang commentary says that for the first line of Bi, something unlooked-for turns out well. A sincere beginning brings unexpected reward.

Reach out on a basis of honesty, with sincerity full as an earthen jar. What follows is a gain you did not plan for.

Build the relationship or partnership genuinely. However plain it looks at the start, sincerity tends to return more than expected.

Is there an ordinary-looking but genuine connection whose sincerity you have not yet expressed?

Six in the second place: Joining from within. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

The Xiang commentary says that holding together from within means one does not lose oneself. Sincerity moves outward from the inside, without flattery.

Closeness that comes from inside you rather than from surface accommodation. It is genuine outward movement, and holding to it is favourable.

You do not need to perform or shape yourself to someone else's preferences. Start from what you actually feel and stay yourself.

Is this relationship coming from real feeling, or have you lost some of yourself in accommodating?

Six in the third place: Joining the wrong people.

The Xiang commentary asks whether holding together with the wrong people is not painful indeed.

Binding yourself closely to people of poor character or poor fit is a choice to regret. That kind of closeness only causes damage.

If someone you have grown close to leaves you uneasy, or the relationship does not feel healthy, that deserves serious rethinking.

Is there someone you know you should not be this close to, and what has stopped you changing it?

Six in the fourth place: Joining outwardly. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

The Xiang commentary says holding together outwardly with the worthy means following those above. Correctness here is favourable.

Reach outward to people of ability and character, following better guidance with an honest attitude. This is the favourable direction.

Go towards people wiser or more capable than you, and ask for guidance or collaboration. That is the right move here.

Who is worth approaching and learning from, and what has kept you from acting?

Nine in the fifth place: Open joining. The king drives game on three sides, letting animals ahead escape. The townspeople need no warning. Good fortune.

The Xiang commentary attributes the good fortune of open holding together to the central and correct position. Letting the game ahead escape means releasing what turns away and keeping what comes willingly.

Gather people openly and fairly. Let those who want to leave go, and those who want to stay gather of their own accord. No coercion; balanced treatment draws people in.

You do not need to force anyone to join or agree. Act openly and fairly; the people who genuinely value it stay, and those who leave need no chasing.

Is there any pressure in how you build relationships, and what would happen if you used only sincerity?

Six at the top: Joining without a head. Misfortune.

The Xiang commentary says holding together without a beginning has no ending either. Without taking the lead, nothing comes to completion.

Hesitating instead of making the first move lets the opening pass. No firm bond forms, and the position is a poor one.

If you keep waiting for the other side to reach out and never make a move yourself, you may lose a relationship or opportunity that mattered.

Which connection have you been waiting on, and are you willing to take the first step?

Quick Quiz

Which is the only solid line in Bi?
  • The fifth line
  • The second line
  • The sixth line
View answers and explanations for every option

The fifth line (Correct) : Correct. Upper Kan's solid middle line is the fifth of the whole hexagram.

The second line : Shi has a solid second line. Bi's second is broken.

The sixth line : The sixth is broken. A solid sixth line with all others broken forms Bo.

How are Bi and Shi related?
  • The same Kun and Kan, with their positions reversed
  • They differ in three lines
  • Bi is Shi with its bottom line flipped
View answers and explanations for every option

The same Kun and Kan trigrams, with upper and lower positions reversed (Correct) : Correct. Shi has Kan below and Kun above; Bi has Kun below and Kan above.

They differ in three lines : They have equal numbers of yin and yang lines but different positions.

Bi is Shi with its bottom line flipped : Changing flips one line. These two differ by moving entire trigrams.

Why is the fifth position often treated as significant?
  • It is the middle of the upper trigram: central and high at once
  • Because it is always yang
  • Because it is the topmost line
View answers and explanations for every option

It is central within the upper trigram, both centered and above (Correct) : Correct. This structural position should be considered separately from whether the line is yin or yang.

Because it is always yang : The fifth line can be yin or yang. Shi's fifth is broken.

Because it is the topmost line : The very top is the sixth line.
