Lin · Approach

Approach others sincerely, lead with tolerance, act with favorable momentum, and keep a way back open at the peak

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Lin: Supreme success. Steadfast correctness is beneficial. By the eighth month, misfortune.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Approach: the firm gradually grows. Joyful and receptive, the firm is central and responsive; great success through correctness is Heaven's way. By the eighth month, misfortune, because decline is not far away.” Commentary on the Image: “Earth above the lake: Approach. The noble person teaches with inexhaustible care and embraces and protects the people without limit.” The Judgment describes yang growing gradually, joyful and receptive, enabling great success. The eighth-month warning limits this opportunity: decline follows the peak. The Image describes leadership through endless teaching and boundless tolerance. (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Lin has Dui below and Kun above, Earth above the lake, symbolizing approaching those below from a higher position. Two firm yang lines grow upward from below, so the judgment grants supreme success and beneficial steadfastness: momentum is rising. Yet “by the eighth month, misfortune” explicitly limits its duration; decline follows the peak. The lines move through responsive, sweet, personal, wise, and generous approaches, emphasizing sincerity and tolerance rather than high position.

The judgment gives supreme success and beneficial steadfastness, while many lines mention good fortune and no blame. Rising yang is clearly positive, hence the rating: Favorable. Yet the explicit eighth-month warning, explained as approaching decline, makes this a time-limited opportunity. The third line also warns that sweet-talking approach brings no benefit, so this is not the highest rating. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Do: Stay close to actual conditions, lead patiently, and build lasting systems while conditions favor you. Avoid: Condescension, seeking only short-term expansion, or forgetting that strong momentum changes.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Kun above, Dui below: two solid lines at the very bottom, pushing upward.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Lin.
  • Say what it means for both solid lines to sit at the bottom.
  • Work out that Approaching with its halves swapped is Gathering.

The lower trigram of Lin is Dui: solid first and second lines with a broken third. The upper is Kun, all broken. Only the bottom two lines of the figure are solid; the other four are broken.

Both solid lines sit at the very bottom, with four yin lines above. Structurally this reads as something newly risen that is still low down: the force is real, the position is not yet high. Tradition reads it as approaching, and the point is that it is early rather than impressive. The risk in this structure is mistaking early progress for arrival.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a new practice has just been adopted by two teams while the rest of the organisation has said nothing. Lin describes that early structure; it does not say the practice spreads.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Nine at the beginning: Responsive approach. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Responsive approach and steadfast good fortune: purpose and conduct are correct.” Approaching people and situations responsively while remaining correct brings fortune because both intention and behavior follow the right path. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

At the beginning, approach people and situations with sincere responsiveness. Staying correct brings good fortune, grounded in rightful motives rather than clever tactics.

When taking over a team or beginning a relationship, connect through sincere concern and explain your position and boundaries. This works better than rushing to display authority.

Can your reasons for approaching this person or undertaking withstand your own scrutiny?

Nine in the second place: Responsive approach. Good fortune; nothing unfavorable.

Commentary on the Image: “Responsive approach brings good fortune and nothing unfavorable; it has not simply obeyed commands.” Interpretations of the final phrase vary, but generally emphasize retaining one's own principles rather than blindly following outside instructions. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

As sincerity deepens, the situation becomes fortunate with nothing unfavorable. The Image stresses that this line retains independent judgment within sincerity rather than merely obeying commands.

Even when cooperation goes well, maintain independent judgment. Explain your reasons honestly when requests are unreasonable, rather than agreeing to everything to preserve the atmosphere.

Now that things are going well, how much of your own judgment do you retain?

Six in the third place: Sweet approach. Nothing is beneficial.

Commentary on the Image: “Sweet approach comes from an inappropriate position. Once concerned about it, the fault will not endure.” Approaching through pleasing sweetness reflects an unsuitable position, but recognizing the problem and correcting it prevents lasting fault. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Leading only through kind words and sweet inducements brings no benefit. Yet the text adds that genuinely recognizing the problem and caring enough to correct it prevents prolonged fault.

If pleasing others has become your way of maintaining relationships or leading a team, return to factual explanations and clear standards, acknowledging that your earlier method was unsuitable.

Which recent instance of cooperation bought with sweeteners should instead be met with honesty?

Six in the fourth place: Arriving in person to approach. No blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Personal approach brings no blame because the position is appropriate.” Coming in person allows direct contact with those below, and the fitting position explains the absence of fault. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Being personally present and facing reality directly brings no fault. The point is to avoid directing things from a distance.

When a situation is hard to explain, visit the site and speak directly with those involved. This handles matters better than relying only on reports or secondhand accounts.

Have you seen this yourself, or only heard about it from others?

Six in the fifth place: Wise approach, fitting for a great ruler. Good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “What is fitting for a great ruler means acting through the middle way.” Wise leadership brings good fortune by remaining balanced in action. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Lead wisely, recognize people's strengths, and delegate rather than doing everything yourself. This fits the limits of a senior role and brings good fortune through balance without bias.

Give suitable work to suitable people while holding direction and principles yourself. You need not intervene personally in every detail.

Which task you are carrying personally should actually go to someone better suited?

Six at the top: Generous approach. Good fortune and no blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Good fortune in generous approach: the purpose remains within.” Generous treatment of those below brings fortune without fault because one's purpose remains with the people and affairs inside. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Even at the hexagram's highest position, treat those below generously. This brings fortune without fault and shows that generosity is both the culmination and foundation of approach.

Even after leaving the front line, support former partners generously, passing on experience without competing for credit.

Do you remain equally generous toward people who can no longer help you?

Quick Quiz

Which two lines of Lin are solid?
  • The first and the second
  • The fifth and the sixth
  • The second and the fifth
View answers and explanations for every option

The first and second lines (Correct) : Correct. Dui's two solid lines are at the very bottom; all lines from the third onward are broken.

The fifth and the sixth : Two solid lines at the top describe the reverse arrangement seen in Guan; Lin's upper trigram has three broken lines.

The second and the fifth : Those two positions are each at the center of a trigram, which is not Lin's arrangement.

What does it mean structurally that both solid lines are at the bottom?
  • The force is real but still low down: it is early
  • It means the outcome will improve
  • It means the four lines above do nothing
View answers and explanations for every option

The strength is real, but its position is low and the timing early (Correct) : Correct. This hexagram concerns the beginning stage rather than outward momentum.

It means the outcome will improve : Hexagrams describe structures and do not guarantee outcomes.

It means the four lines above do nothing : All six lines belong to the structure; the four upper broken lines include the entire Kun trigram.

Which trigram is the upper half of Lin?
  • Kun · Earth (three broken lines)
  • Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above)
  • Gen (two broken below, one solid on top)
View answers and explanations for every option

Kun · Earth (three broken lines) (Correct) : Correct. The fourth, fifth, and sixth lines are all broken.

Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above) : Dui is Lin's lower trigram.

Gen (two broken below, one solid on top) : Gen's top line is solid; Lin's sixth line is broken.
