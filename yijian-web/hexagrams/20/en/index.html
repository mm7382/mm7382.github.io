Guan · Contemplation

Observe and reflect, put yourself in order, see the whole, and understand before acting

Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Guan: Hands washed, but offerings not yet presented. Sincerity inspires reverent attention.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Great contemplation is above, receptive and penetrating, central and correct in observing all under Heaven. Hands washed, offerings not yet presented, with sincerity and reverence: those below observe and are transformed. Observe Heaven's spiritual way and the seasons do not err. The sage teaches through this spiritual way, and all under Heaven submit.” Commentary on the Image: “Wind moves over Earth: Contemplation. The ancient kings inspected the regions, observed the people, and established teaching.” The Judgment describes centered, correct observation by leaders, whose example naturally transforms those below. The Image illustrates instruction grounded in visiting regions and observing people's lives. (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Guan has Kun below and Xun above. Wind travels everywhere over Earth, symbolizing seeing and being seen. Its judgment uses ritual: solemn washing is complete, but offerings have not yet been presented. Sincere reverence is fullest at this point, naturally inspiring respect in observers. Real influence comes from an attitude others see rather than a performance staged for them. The lines move from childish surface observation and peering through a crack to examining one's actions and a nation's excellence, repeatedly showing that shallow sight narrows one's path.

The judgment gives no verdict of good or bad fortune, only a sincerely reverent state worthy of contemplation. The lines emphasize observation and reflection: the first line's “regret for the noble person” and the second's “peering” show narrow vision's limits. The fifth and top nines give the noble person “no blame,” rather than gain, and the top Image adds that the purpose is not yet at peace. The focus is examination and preparation rather than effective action, hence the rating: Neutral. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Do: Observe the whole and your role first, then build trust through consistent action. Avoid: Rushing to judgment from appearances or demanding others change while you do not.

Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Xun above, Kun below: the two solid lines sit at the very top, looking down.

  • Name the upper and lower trigram of Guan.
  • Explain why solid lines above read as observing.
  • Point out that Contemplating and Ascending use the same two trigrams in the other order.

The lower trigram of Guan is Kun, all broken; the upper is Xun, with a broken fourth line and solid fifth and sixth. Only the top two lines of the figure are solid. It is the vertical mirror of Lin.

The solid lines are at the top with four yin lines beneath. A high position over a broad base reads structurally as seeing the whole. The hexagram is about the vantage point, not the conclusion: height lets you see far, but what you see is still only how things are now, not what will happen.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a new lead spends the first two weeks only reading the data and issuing no instructions. Guan describes that structure of gaining a vantage point; it does not say the later decisions are right.

The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

Six at the beginning: Childlike observation. No blame for a petty person; regret for the noble person.

Commentary on the Image: “The first six observes like a child: this is the petty person's way.” Looking only at surfaces incurs no blame for someone of limited understanding, but is regrettable for a noble person with responsibilities. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Judging only from the surface may not be a fault for someone with little responsibility, but such shallow observation is a deficiency for someone accountable.

On matters you are responsible for, do not speak solely from first impressions or fragments of information. Gather the crucial facts before commenting.

Is your view of this matter based on complete information, or only its most conspicuous corner?

Six in the second place: Peering through a crack. A woman's steadfastness is beneficial.

Commentary on the Image: “Peering and a woman's steadfastness can still be shameful.” Peering through a doorway limits vision; the Image finds narrow observation inadequate even with steadfastness. This line reflects gender conditions in ancient society. Today, read it as a description of restricted perspective rather than a modern value judgment. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Peering through a gap reveals only a fragment. The line suggests that remaining thus may be harmless, but the Image still identifies narrow vision itself as a limitation.

If you can only learn about something indirectly, look for a chance to participate or ask those involved. Do not turn assembled fragments into a final conclusion.

Could you see it directly and clearly, but are choosing to watch from afar?

Six in the third place: Observe my life and actions; advance or withdraw.

Commentary on the Image: “Observing my life and actions to advance or withdraw means not losing the Way.” Examining your own conduct to decide whether to advance or retreat keeps you on the right path. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Turn observation toward yourself and decide whether to advance or retreat based on actual results. Such self-reflection keeps you on the right path.

Before deciding whether to keep investing, honestly review your actual results over this period, then decide whether to increase your commitment or stop.

Looking only at what you have actually produced, does the result tell you to advance or withdraw?

Six in the fourth place: Observe the nation's splendor. It is beneficial to be the king's guest.

Commentary on the Image: “Observing the nation's splendor means valuing the guest.” Seeing a country's cultural excellence makes an audience with the king as an honored guest beneficial. The Image emphasizes that honored guest's position. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Recognize a group's true excellence and participate as an honored guest. The focus is identifying an environment worth joining.

When considering a new organization or circle, observe its actual culture and working methods first. Join as a collaborator if you identify with them.

What do you most admire about the place you want to join?

Nine in the fifth place: Observe my life and actions. No blame for the noble person.

Commentary on the Image: “Observing my life and actions means observing the people.” For a leader, examining personal conduct means examining the people's condition, because those below reflect what those above do. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Reflecting on your conduct from a high position brings the noble person no blame. The Image says that to know how you are doing, observe what your influence has made of the people around you.

To assess your way of leading, observe your team's or family's actual condition. It often echoes your long-term behavior.

What aspect of your conduct is reflected in the people around you today?

Nine at the top: Observe their life and actions. No blame for the noble person.

Commentary on the Image: “Observing their life and actions: the purpose is not yet at peace.” Outside the active governing role, one observes others and incurs no blame through upright conduct, yet still cares about worldly affairs. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

No longer in charge, one observes others' actions and remains blameless through upright conduct. Yet the Image notes lingering concerns that have not been released.

After leaving a role, channel continuing concern into offering experience when needed, rather than commenting on every decision.

Does the undertaking you cannot release still need you, or do you still need it?

Quick Quiz

How are Guan and Lin related?
  • The solid lines are at opposite ends
  • They are the same
  • Guan comes from flipping one line of Lin
View answers and explanations for every option

The solid lines occupy opposite upper and lower positions (Correct) : Correct. Lin's two solid lines are at the bottom, while Guan's are at the top.

They are the same : Different arrangements make different hexagrams. Equal numbers of yin and yang lines do not make them identical.

Guan comes from flipping one line of Lin : Four positions differ between them; this is not a single line change.

Why do solid lines above read as observing?
  • A high position over a broad base can see the whole
  • Because the upper trigram is Li, which is brightness
  • Because all six lines are yang
View answers and explanations for every option

A high position and broad base provide a view of the whole (Correct) : Correct. This concerns relative positions, not a guarantee that conclusions are right.

Because the upper trigram is Li, which is brightness : Guan's upper trigram is Xun, not Li.

Because all six lines are yang : Guan has only two solid lines.

Which trigram is the lower half of Guan?
  • Kun · Earth (three broken lines)
  • Xun · Wind (one broken line below, two solid above)
  • Zhen (one solid below, two broken above)
View answers and explanations for every option

Kun · Earth (three broken lines) (Correct) : Correct. The first through third lines are all broken.

Xun · Wind (one broken line below, two solid above) : Xun is Guan's upper trigram.

Zhen (one solid below, two broken above) : Zhen's first line is solid; Guan's is broken.
